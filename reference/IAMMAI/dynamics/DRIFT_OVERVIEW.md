# IAMMAI Drift Overview

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded drift concept for IAMMAI.

Its job is to explain what drift means in this architecture, why drift becomes relevant once the body repeats and varies, how drift differs from lawful variation, adaptation, regulation, and collapse, what drift threatens, and what drift does and does not imply.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- a mathematical derivation
- a behavioral blame model
- a replacement for failure handling
- a full drift taxonomy
- a public narrative about system decline

It is a bounded architectural document for the first drift layer.

## 2. First Principle

Drift is meaningful only relative to a prior invariant.

Drift does not create law; it is measured against law.  
Drift becomes relevant because the body already repeats under variation.  
Drift must be named without collapsing immediately into panic or denial.

In IAMMAI terms, drift is not free-floating deviation. It is deviation relative to constitutional integrity conditions that are already prior, already governing, and already visible in repeated lawful motion.

## 3. Why Drift Is Needed Now

One successful run does not yet create a drift question.

A single lawful cycle can prove that bounded motion is possible, but it does not yet show whether repeated motion remains close to lawful form or begins to accumulate deviation.

Repeated runs, visible failures, controlled variation, and continuity do create that question.

Once the body repeats under bounded variation, it needs a way to distinguish lawful variation from deviation that matters. Without that distinction, every difference looks suspicious or every deviation looks harmless.

Drift is therefore needed now because the body has already moved enough for deviation across turns to become a real architectural issue rather than a speculative one.

## 4. What Drift Means Here

Drift in IAMMAI means motion away from lawful integrity conditions over repeated turns.

At a bounded architectural level, drift includes:

- deviation in sequencing
- deviation in distinction
- deviation in preservation
- deviation in relation
- accumulated slippage that does not yet necessarily equal total collapse

This is not yet total failure and not yet full collapse. It is the pattern in which repeated motion begins to pressure what the body must keep lawful and distinct.

## 5. Drift vs Lawful Variation

Variation is bounded difference within the same slice class.

Drift is deviation that pressures or weakens lawful integrity.

Not all variation is drift.

Drift begins when change threatens what must remain invariant or distinct. Controlled variation changes bounded matter content while preserving slice class, runtime role family, artifact family, access boundary, and visible failure posture. Drift begins when repeated difference starts to erode those preserved boundaries rather than merely exercising them.

## 6. Drift vs Failure

One failed run is not automatically drift.

Drift may exist without immediate total failure.

Failure is an event or outcome. Drift is a patterned deviation over time or across turns.

A body can fail visibly while remaining lawfully bounded. It can also appear operational while drift accumulates in ways that have not yet produced a single decisive breakdown. These are different questions and must remain distinct.

## 7. Drift vs Collapse

Drift is not yet total collapse.

Drift may be recoverable.

Collapse is a more severe breakdown of lawful distinction or integrity.

Naming drift early matters because drift is one of the ways a body may move toward collapse without having fully arrived there. Early recognition of drift may therefore preserve lawful form before more severe non-collapse protections fail.

## 8. Drift vs Adaptation

Adaptation is not just any change.

Adaptation would need to remain lawful relative to integrity.

Unbounded deviation is not adaptation.

Drift may reveal the need for regulation before adaptation is justified. The fact that motion is changing does not by itself mean the body is adapting lawfully. It may instead mean that the body is moving away from form in a way that first needs to be understood and regulated.

## 9. What Drift Threatens

Drift threatens, at minimum:

- sequence integrity
- state distinction
- truth versus decision separation
- witness, validation, and governance separation
- canonical versus derivative boundary
- continuity truthfulness
- non-overwrite posture

These threats matter because IAMMAI depends on preserving explicit distinction under motion. Drift becomes serious when repeated deviation begins to blur these lines, soften failure visibility, weaken separation between roles, or make continuity appear smoother than the preserved artifacts actually justify.

## 10. What Drift Must Not Become

Drift must not become:

- a vague anxiety category
- a default moral accusation
- a label for every visible failure
- an invocation detached from constitutional invariant
- proof that the whole system is false

It must also not become a rhetorical shortcut by which lawful variation is misnamed as danger or by which visible failure is exaggerated into total invalidity.

## 11. Relation to Regulation and Stability

Regulation is the response question: how lawful motion is preserved under pressure.

Drift is the deviation question: how motion moves away from lawful integrity.

Stability will later ask what it means for the body to remain stably lawful over time.

These concepts are related but not identical. Regulation asks how the body preserves form. Drift asks how it departs from form. Stability will later ask under what conditions lawful form persists despite repeated motion and pressure.

## 12. Summary

This document defines drift as the first bounded deviation concept of IAMMAI because the body now repeats, varies, fails visibly, and accumulates continuity across turns.

Its purpose is to make deviation nameable without confusing it with lawful variation, visible failure, total collapse, or lawful adaptation, while keeping constitutional integrity prior to every such judgment.
