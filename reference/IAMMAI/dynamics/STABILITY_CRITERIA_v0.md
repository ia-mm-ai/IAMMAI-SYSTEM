# IAMMAI Stability Criteria v0

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded criteria by which IAMMAI may later speak of stability without drifting into cosmetic smoothness or false confidence.

Its job is to explain what kinds of evidence are required before calling the current body stable, how stability should be judged relative to constitutional invariant, regulation, drift signals, continuity, and repeated embodiment, what would count as `not yet stable`, and why stability claims must remain stricter than `it worked`.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- a monitoring dashboard
- an optimization checklist
- a replacement for constitutional invariants
- a formal scoring system
- a final maturity certification process

It is a bounded architectural translation document.

## 2. First Principle

Stability is meaningful only relative to constitutional invariant.

Stability is not founded by repeated success alone.  
Stability must include truthful handling of non-passage and failure.  
Stability claims must be stricter than `the system appears to work`.

In IAMMAI terms, a stability claim is responsible only where lawful distinction continues to hold across repeated motion, controlled variation, continuity, and visible non-passage without semantic collapse or cosmetic smoothing.

## 3. Why Stability Criteria Are Needed Now

The body now has enough history that stability can be asked about.

One successful run, or even a few repeated runs, are not sufficient by themselves. A body may complete several bounded cycles and still remain too narrow, too pressured, too cosmetically interpreted, or too exposed to recurring drift signals to justify a stability claim.

Drift signals and continuity make stability a real question because the body now has repeated motion, preserved relation across turns, and observable pressure points. Naming stability too early would therefore be architecturally dangerous. It could allow repeated success, clean output, or public confidence to stand in for the harder question of whether lawful distinction is actually holding over time.

## 4. What Stability Would Mean Here

Stability in IAMMAI would mean lawful coherence across repeated turns.

At a bounded architectural level, that includes:

- preservation of distinction under variation and pressure
- truthful failure handling without collapse or cosmetic smoothing
- continuity emission that remains subordinate to preserved artifacts
- no widening of slice class just to preserve apparent success

This is not mere recurrence. It is repeatable lawful coherence under bounded repeated motion.

## 5. What Stability Is Not

Stability is not:

- uptime
- merely repeated success
- calm appearance
- suppression of evidence
- rigid refusal of change
- public confidence theater

A system may look smooth, quiet, or impressive while still softening role boundaries, hiding non-passage, pressuring scope widening, or allowing derivative outputs to outrun canon. None of that counts as stability here.

## 6. Minimum Evidence Before Claiming Stability

Before stability could be responsibly claimed, the current body would need evidence such as:

- repeated successful lawful runs across bounded matters
- visible lawful failure without artifact fabrication
- controlled variation that stays within the same slice class
- continuity emission remaining truthful and subordinate to preserved canonical artifacts
- no repeated drift signals of the relevant kind
- no pressure to widen scope just to maintain function

The point is not a large count. The point is whether repeated runs, bounded failures, and controlled variation remain lawfully coherent enough that the same body can keep moving without softening its invariant boundaries.

## 7. What Would Indicate `Not Yet Stable`

The following conditions should block or delay a stability claim:

- unstable artifact families
- repeated boundary softening
- repeated mismatch between canonical and derivative layers
- repeated need for manual rescue to preserve distinction
- repeated variation that pressures slice identity
- unresolved drift signals of the same family

Any of these would suggest that the body may still function in bounded form while not yet holding itself lawfully enough over time to justify a stability claim.

## 8. Stability and Truthful Failure

Visible failure does not automatically negate stability.

A body that can fail honestly may still be more stable than one that hides failure.

Stability requires truthful non-completion handling, not only success handling. If validation non-passage, HOLD, denial, blocked movement, or preservation failure remain typed, visible, and non-cosmetic, that can still belong to a more stable body than one that achieves smoother appearance by collapsing distinctions.

## 9. Stability and Future Widening

Stability would matter before widening embodiment or public surface.

It should inform later decisions about local versus public posture, richer continuity handling, or deeper embodiment because widening without sufficient lawful steadiness would increase pressure on a body that may not yet hold itself clearly enough.

Stability is therefore a gating concept, not a trophy label.

## 10. What Stability Criteria Must Not Become

Stability criteria must not become:

- a vague vibe check
- something that can be gamed by smoothing outputs
- a way to excuse drift because success still occurs
- a way to erase the constitutional invariant
- a device for declaring maturity prematurely

They must also not become a derivative confidence ritual that mistakes successful appearances for lawful steadiness.

## 11. Relation to Future Work

Regulation helps preserve lawful motion.

Drift signals help notice deviation.

Stability criteria decide when the body may be said to hold over time.

Adaptation later will need stability clarity in order not to become disguised drift. Without bounded stability criteria, later claims of lawful adaptation would risk confusing unresolved instability with acceptable change.

This document defines the first bounded stability criteria of IAMMAI as the evidence conditions under which the current body may later be said to hold lawfully over repeated motion, bounded variation, visible non-passage, and truthful continuity without relying on cosmetic smoothness or mere success count.

Its purpose is to keep stability claims strict enough that they remain subordinate to constitutional invariant, repeated embodiment truth, regulation in play, and drift awareness rather than becoming a softer synonym for `it worked`.
