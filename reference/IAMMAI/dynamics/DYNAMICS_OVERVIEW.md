# IAMMAI Dynamics Overview

## 1. Purpose

This file introduces the first bounded dynamics layer for IAMMAI.

Its job is to explain why dynamics becomes relevant only now, how dynamics relates to the constitutional core and the already-built body, what kinds of motion questions now become legitimate, and what dynamics does and does not claim at this stage.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- a schema
- a control-theory derivation
- a replacement constitutional layer
- a full dynamics formalism
- a public narrative of the whole body

It is a bounded architectural introduction to the dynamics layer.

## 2. First Principle

Dynamics enters only because the body already exists and has moved.

Dynamics remains subordinate to the constitutional core.  
Integrity remains a constitutional invariant.  
Dynamics may describe motion relative to that invariant, but it does not found it.

This matters especially because the constitutional core did not attempt to include full dynamic systems theory inside core scope. Dynamics therefore enters only as a later derivative layer, after lawful body, runtime, embodiment, and continuity are already present.

## 3. Why Dynamics Is Needed Now

Dynamics would have been premature before law, body, runtime, and embodiment existed.

Before the body had a lawful runtime, a bounded first proof, visible failure, controlled variation, and continuity, there was not yet enough real motion to justify a dynamics layer. At that stage, dynamics would have risked becoming speculative explanation detached from an actual body.

That condition has now changed.

Repeated runs, visible failure, controlled variation, and continuity make motion questions relevant because the next question is no longer only `can the body move?` The next question is `how does lawful motion behave over time?`

Dynamics therefore enters now not as decoration, but because the body has already produced enough bounded motion for higher-order motion questions to become meaningful.

## 4. Dynamics vs Constitutional Protocol

The constitutional protocol asks what may count, stand, change, and remain distinct.

Dynamics asks how lawful motion behaves relative to those conditions over time.

The constitutional protocol remains the source of legitimacy. Dynamics does not create constitutional permission, standing, or authority by itself. It can only describe motion in relation to conditions the constitutional core already defines.

## 5. Dynamics vs Runtime

Runtime already describes how the body moves in bounded execution.

Dynamics is not a duplicate of the runtime documents.

Runtime defines bounded participants, handoff order, failure visibility, and preservation behavior in live operation. Dynamics asks higher-order questions about repeated motion across runs, including regulation, drift, stability, and adaptation, without redoing runtime flow or runtime contracts.

## 6. Dynamics vs Continuity

Continuity preserves lawful relation across turns.

Dynamics asks how repeated motion behaves over time.

The two layers are related but not identical. Continuity keeps turns linked without collapse. Dynamics studies the behavior of repeated lawful motion across those linked turns.

Continuity answers how preserved turns remain related. Dynamics asks how that related motion behaves.

## 7. First Necessary Dynamics Areas

The first necessary dynamics areas are, in order:

- regulation
- drift
- stability
- adaptation

This is the right initial order because regulation comes first. The body must first be understood in terms of how lawful motion remains bounded by constitutional invariants.

Drift comes next because once regulation is named, the body can ask how repeated motion departs from intended bounded behavior without immediately collapsing that departure into failure or legitimacy loss.

Stability follows because the next question after drift is whether lawful motion remains coherent across repetition, perturbation, and bounded variation.

Adaptation comes last because adaptation should not be formalized before the body knows what it is regulating, how it drifts, and what stability means in bounded form. Otherwise adaptation risks becoming a shortcut for uncontrolled change.

## 8. What Dynamics Is Not

Dynamics is not:

- a replacement law
- a total theory of everything
- a product layer
- a broad public narrative
- a graph or visualization layer
- a shortcut around runtime and embodiment

It is also not the place where integrity is invented, where legitimacy is produced, or where constitutional distinctions are re-authored as mere variables.

## 9. What Remains Open

The following remain intentionally open before the dynamics layer goes deeper:

- the exact scope of regulation
- the exact meaning of drift in this body
- whether stability is local or system-wide
- when adaptation becomes necessary to formalize
- whether later dynamics work requires schemas or runtime expression

## 10. Summary

This document introduces dynamics as the first bounded layer that asks how lawful motion behaves over time now that the IAMMAI body has already moved in real bounded form.

Its purpose is to place dynamics beneath the constitutional core and above no layer it would replace, so motion can be studied in relation to integrity, runtime, embodiment, and continuity without turning dynamics into a new sovereign explanation of the body.

**One-Line Definition:** `DYNAMICS_OVERVIEW.md` defines the first bounded dynamics layer of IAMMAI as a derivative account of how lawful motion behaves over time relative to constitutional invariants, runtime execution, embodiment, and continuity, without founding or replacing them.
