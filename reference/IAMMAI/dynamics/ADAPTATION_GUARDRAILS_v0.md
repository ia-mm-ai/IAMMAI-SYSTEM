# IAMMAI Adaptation Guardrails v0

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded guardrails for adaptation in IAMMAI.

Its job is to explain what adaptation may mean in this architecture, why adaptation is not yet active just because the body can move, what must remain invariant if adaptation ever becomes lawful, how adaptation differs from variation, drift, repair, and opportunistic expansion, and what conditions would need to be true before adaptation could be responsibly claimed or translated into embodiment or runtime.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- an optimization note
- a growth roadmap
- a permission slip for broadening the system
- a runtime implementation plan
- a final adaptation mechanism

It is a bounded architectural guardrail document.

## 2. First Principle

Adaptation is meaningful only relative to a prior invariant.

Adaptation must remain subordinate to constitutional integrity.  
Adaptation is not automatically justified by repeated motion or local success.  
Adaptation must not become a disguised path for lawful erosion.

In IAMMAI terms, the existence of a living body does not by itself authorize reconfiguration. Movement, repetition, visible failure, and continuity make adaptation thinkable. They do not make it lawful by default.

## 3. Why Adaptation Guardrails Are Needed

Adaptation is attractive because a living body under pressure naturally tempts interpretation toward change.

A body that repeats successfully can easily confuse successful variation with permission to mutate. A body that encounters drift pressure can easily confuse relief from pressure with lawful reconfiguration. A body that begins to feel alive can easily overread that fact as justification for broadening.

Adaptation therefore needs stronger guardrails than earlier dynamics concepts. Regulation, drift, and stability can be opened as ways of naming motion, deviation, and steadiness. Adaptation is more dangerous because it sits closest to actual change in form or behavior.

This file exists before adaptation is translated into code or runtime behavior so that the repo does not smuggle structural widening, convenience mutation, or drift-response improvisation under a more dignified name.

## 4. What Adaptation Means Here

Adaptation in IAMMAI means lawful reconfiguration that preserves the constitutional invariant.

At a bounded architectural level, it means:

- change that remains accountable to integrity, distinction, and continuity
- adjustment that does not falsify prior history
- adjustment that does not blur what must remain distinct

This is not generic change. It is only the possibility that the body may one day need to alter some part of its organization or response while still remaining lawfully itself.

## 5. Adaptation vs Lawful Variation

Variation stays within the same bounded slice class.

Adaptation would imply a more meaningful change in how the body organizes or responds.

Not every repeated difference is adaptation.

Controlled variation tests repeatability across bounded matters while preserving slice identity, role family, artifact family, and local execution boundary. Adaptation would ask a different question: whether some lawful change beyond repeated bounded variation is needed without violating what must remain invariant.

## 6. Adaptation vs Drift

Drift is deviation away from lawful integrity over time.

Adaptation is not deviation renamed attractively.

Adaptation must not be used to justify what is actually unregulated drift. If role boundaries soften, continuity outruns canon, slice identity pressures widening, or non-passage gets cosmetically smoothed, that is not adaptation simply because the body remained active while it happened.

## 7. Adaptation vs Repair / Correction

Repair or correction may restore the body to lawful form.

Adaptation would imply lawful change beyond mere restoration.

The two are related but not identical. A repair may bring the body back into clearer conformity with its present lawful form. Adaptation would only become relevant if the body could not remain lawfully itself without some bounded reconfiguration that still preserved the invariant.

## 8. What Must Remain Invariant

If adaptation ever becomes lawful, the following must remain invariant:

- constitutional integrity
- lawful distinction between roles and states
- anti-overwrite and append-oriented continuity
- canonical versus derivative boundary
- truthful failure visibility
- subordination of dynamics to law

These are not negotiable adaptation inputs. They are the prior conditions under which adaptation could be judged at all.

## 9. What Conditions Would Need To Be True Before Adaptation Could Be Responsibly Claimed

Before adaptation could be responsibly claimed, the body would need conditions such as:

- stronger stability evidence
- clearer drift signals and clearer regulation responses
- more than one local slice or more embodied history
- trustworthy continuity across turns
- confidence that the proposed change is not just pressure relief or convenience widening

These conditions should be read conservatively. They are not signs that adaptation should already happen. They are thresholds beneath which adaptation claims should remain suspended.

## 10. What Adaptation Must Not Become

Adaptation must not become:

- a prestige term for mutation
- a justification for broadening scope prematurely
- a way to erase history or rewrite prior proofs
- something claimed merely because the body feels alive
- a bypass around constitutional clarification

It must also not become a softer vocabulary for drift, a narrative reward for repeated success, or a rationale for expanding embodiment beyond what the current lawful body can yet carry.

## 11. Relation to Future Work

Adaptation remains the most guarded dynamics concept.

Later work may clarify whether any parts deserve embodiment or runtime translation. Until then, adaptation remains bounded conceptually and under guardrails.

That means future work may ask whether some lawful reconfiguration ever becomes necessary, but it should do so only after stronger regulation understanding, clearer drift handling, more defensible stability evidence, and continuity that remains trustworthy under repeated motion.

## 12. Summary

This document defines the first bounded guardrails for adaptation in IAMMAI as the conditions that keep adaptation subordinate to constitutional integrity, lawful distinction, truthful continuity, and append-oriented history rather than letting it become a polite name for drift, convenience change, or premature broadening.

Its purpose is to ensure that adaptation remains the most restricted dynamics concept now entering the repo: conceptually opened, but not yet activated, operationalized, or justified merely because the body has moved.

**One-Line Definition:** `ADAPTATION_GUARDRAILS_v0.md` defines the first bounded guardrails for IAMMAI adaptation as the requirement that any future lawful reconfiguration remain subordinate to constitutional integrity, preserve role and state distinction, avoid overwrite, resist convenience widening, and wait for stronger stability and continuity evidence before any translation into embodiment or runtime.
