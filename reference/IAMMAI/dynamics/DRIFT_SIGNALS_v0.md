# IAMMAI Drift Signals v0

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded set of drift signals for IAMMAI.

Its job is to explain what observable conditions would count as early drift signals in the current body, how drift signals differ from lawful variation, isolated failure, and total collapse, what the body should watch without panic or denial, and how drift signals relate to regulation, continuity, and future stability claims.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- a monitoring spec
- an alerting system
- a blame document
- a complete drift-detection framework
- an automated response policy

It is a bounded architectural translation document.

## 2. First Principle

Drift signals are meaningful only relative to constitutional invariant and lawful body expectations.

A drift signal is not yet full drift, collapse, or verdict.  
Drift signals exist to preserve clarity early, not to trigger panic.  
The body should be able to notice deviation without cosmetic smoothing or premature closure.

In IAMMAI terms, a drift signal is an early observable sign that repeated motion may be starting to pressure invariant boundaries, role distinctions, continuity truthfulness, or slice identity. It is a prompt for clearer naming, not a final judgment.

## 3. Why Drift Signals Are Needed Now

Repeated runs and controlled variation make early deviation legible.

Once the body moves across more than one bounded run, repeated patterns can begin to appear that would not have been visible in a single proof. The body therefore needs a way to notice slippage before collapse, rather than waiting until distinction has already been materially weakened.

One failure is not the same as a drift signal pattern.

Drift signals matter because they help preserve regulation and future stability claims. If the body cannot tell the difference between one honest bounded non-passage and a recurring pressure toward semantic softening, then later claims of lawful steadiness will remain too vague to trust.

## 4. What Counts as a Drift Signal in the Current Body

The first bounded drift-signal families in the current body include:

- repeated role-boundary softening
- repeated pressure toward false continuity
- repeated artifact instability across the same slice class
- repeated mismatch between canonical and derivative outputs
- repeated need to widen scope in order to `make it work`
- repeated collapse of distinction between success, failure, hold, denial, and non-passage
- repeated continuity emission that begins to outrun preserved artifacts
- repeated variation that pressures slice identity rather than staying within it

In practical terms, these signals would appear as patterns such as:

- the same slice repeatedly needing role shortcuts, missing prerequisites, or convenience handoffs
- derivative summaries or outputs repeatedly appearing cleaner than canonical reality
- the same bounded run class repeatedly producing unstable artifact expectations
- controlled variation repeatedly pushing toward new matter categories, broader access, or richer ontology rather than staying inside the same bounded class
- continuity pressure beginning to favor narrative linkage over preserved canonical basis

These are signals because they indicate repeated pressure on lawful distinction, not because any single instance automatically proves drift.

## 5. Drift Signals vs Isolated Failure

One visible failed run is not drift by itself.

Honest non-passage can belong to a healthy body.

Drift signals emerge when deviation begins to pattern across turns or across bounded variation. A single validation failure, witness absence, governance denial, HOLD, or registry preservation failure may be a truthful localized event rather than evidence of broader slippage.

Truthful failure reporting is often anti-drift, not evidence of drift, because it preserves clarity instead of smoothing non-passage into false success.

## 6. Drift Signals vs Lawful Variation

Lawful variation stays within the same slice class and preserves the same artifact family and role distinctions.

Drift signals appear when variation starts pressuring invariant boundaries, execution scope, or lawful distinctions.

Not every difference is a warning.

In the current body, lawful controlled variation changes bounded matter while preserving slice identity, runtime role family, failure visibility, and canonical artifact expectations. Drift signals appear when repeated variation starts demanding broader scope, looser role boundaries, or softer success criteria in order to continue.

## 7. Drift Signals vs Collapse

Drift signals are early indicators.

Collapse is a more severe breakdown of lawful distinction or integrity.

The purpose of naming signals is to avoid both denial and overreaction. Early signals should make pressure more legible before the body reaches a state in which canonical versus derivative confusion, role collapse, continuity fiction, or overwrite pressure has already become severe.

## 8. Where Drift Signals Are Most Likely to Appear First

In the current body, drift signals are most likely to appear first at the following pressure points:

- controlled variation runner behavior
- canonical versus derivative separation
- continuity emission boundaries
- success or failure judgment
- pressure to broaden the first slice
- pressure to rewrite history instead of appending new turns

These are the places where the living body already operates close to important anti-collapse boundaries: repeatability without widening, truthful derivative reporting without replacement of canon, continuity without narrative smoothing, and visible failure without embarrassment-driven rewriting.

## 9. What To Do With a Drift Signal

A drift signal should trigger:

- observation
- clearer bounded naming
- possible pause
- possible regulation tightening
- not immediate mythology or total closure

The architectural response is to make the pressure explicit, determine whether it is isolated or patterned, and decide whether the current slice should pause, tighten, or remain unchanged. A drift signal does not by itself authorize a broadened redesign, retrospective rewriting, or premature declaration of collapse.

## 10. What Drift Signals Must Not Become

Drift signals must not become:

- a label for every imperfection
- moral verdicts
- excuses to rewrite history
- proof that the body is false
- invocations detached from invariant and actual repeated deviation

They must also not become a convenient way to dramatize honest non-passage, lawful incompleteness, or bounded local roughness into something larger than the current evidence supports.

## 11. Relation to Future Work

Regulation in play helps name what is already preserving lawful motion.

Drift signals help identify where that preservation may be weakening.

Stability work later will need these signals to avoid becoming cosmetic, because lawful steadiness cannot be claimed without some disciplined way to notice emerging deviation.

Adaptation later must not be confused with drift. A detected signal may reveal pressure that needs regulation, pause, or later lawful change, but the signal itself is not permission to mutate the body by convenience.

## 12. Summary

This document defines the first bounded drift signals of IAMMAI as early observable signs that repeated motion, controlled variation, continuity handling, or derivative pressure may be starting to weaken lawful distinction in the current body.

Its purpose is to make early deviation nameable without confusing it with isolated failure, lawful variation, or total collapse, so the body can remain clear-eyed, non-panicked, and regulation-capable as repeated runs continue.
