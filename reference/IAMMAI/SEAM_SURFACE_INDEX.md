## 4. Recurring Seam Laws

The following recurring seam laws are already evidenced across the current body.

- Source is not erased by emergence. Root remains frozen source-side lineage even where `v1/` now stands as a real derivation line.
- Continued contact is not the same as continued containment. `v1/` remains beside root, not a hidden cleanup of root inside a cleaner present.
- Derivative receipt is not the same as source authority. What is inherited, referenced, preserved, or re-derived must be named explicitly.
- Crossing requires admissibility. Source selection, continuity succession, and later preserved relation are lawful only where the source class and relation class are admissible.
- Anchor relation must not be replaced by succession convenience. Continuity remains subordinate to preserved canonical reality rather than to generic sequence.
- Preservation must not redefine identity. Preservation preserves what stood as body, execution relation, or preservation context; it does not merge those classes by grouped storage.
- Readability must not flatten rank. Snapshot and report surfaces may clarify preserved bodies, but they do not replace the ranked bodies they describe.
- Later clarification does not rewrite earlier standing objects. Transfer-account entries, current-state clarification, constitutional clarification, and standing review all follow additive posture rather than retroactive smoothing.

## 5. What the Seam Is Not

The seam should not be mistaken for:

- merger
- silent overwrite
- flat translation
- convenience bridge
- automatic inheritance
- generic storage or retrieval grouping
- atmospheric continuity
- a full middleware layer already declared and closed

The seam is a lawful distinction surface. It prevents collapse. It is not a license to speak as though every middle relation has already been fully constituted as its own finished layer.
