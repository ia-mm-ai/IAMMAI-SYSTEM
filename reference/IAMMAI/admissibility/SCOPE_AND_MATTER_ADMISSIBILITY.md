# IAMMAI Scope and Matter Admissibility

## 1. Purpose

This file defines scope and matter admissibility as the bounded admissibility subdomain governing when material already admitted for handling may or may not lawfully count for the matter, relation, or bounded question now being claimed inside the IAMMAI body.

Its job is to clarify the lawful difference between admitted handling, matter fit, relation fit, scoped handling, and the later domains that may follow after scope without letting those later domains collapse backward into scope itself.

It does not:

- rewrite the constitutional protocol
- create a new Scope section
- rewrite `runtime/VALIDATOR_CONTRACT.md`
- rewrite `admissibility/SOURCE_ADMISSIBILITY.md`
- define a canon admission procedure
- declare standing
- define a transition runtime flow map
- define a source-selection note
- define a generic relevance policy
- act as a project-management note, README, or manifesto

This file is additive. It does not replace constitutional phase order or other ranked surfaces. It binds one admissibility domain more explicitly inside the body already standing.

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

The constitutional and local roots for scope and matter admissibility already exist.

The body now benefits from a clearer bounded file for this subdomain because the repository has become dense enough that accepted material, preserved lineage, readable residue, naming resemblance, or apparent thematic proximity could otherwise begin to counterfeit lawful matter fit if the distinction remains under-specified outside those roots.

That risk is now practical:

- something may be lawfully admitted for handling and still fail to fit the present matter
- preserved or readable material may appear relevant by atmosphere without being lawfully in scope
- naming resemblance or adjacency may begin to impersonate matter fit
- proof-bearing or intense material may appear to matter everywhere once it has entered handling

This file exists to reduce that risk by bounded clarification, not by rewriting the constitutional source or the local matter-coherence posture that already carry the root.

## 4. What Scope and Matter Admissibility Answers

Scope and matter admissibility answer the bounded question:

**Even if something has lawfully entered structured handling, is it admissible to this matter, relation, or bounded question?**

This is a scope question.

It is not yet:

- a truth question
- a threshold question
- a standing question
- a consequence question
- a source-selection question
- a transition-legitimacy question

Scope and matter admissibility authorize bounded matter-relevance posture in lawful form. They do not by themselves decide what later phases will conclude.

## 5. Accepted Material, Matter Fit, and Scoped Material

These must remain distinct.

### 5.1 Accepted material

Accepted material is material that has already passed intake and may lawfully enter structured handling.

Accepted material is not matter fit by default. Admission for handling means the material may be processed; it does not mean the material already counts for the matter now at issue.

### 5.2 Matter fit

Matter fit is the bounded question of whether accepted material belongs to the matter under review rather than merely being available, nearby, intense, or superficially similar.

Matter fit is narrower than admitted handling. It asks whether the material can lawfully count for this matter at all.

### 5.3 Relation fit

Relation fit is the bounded question of whether the way the material is being used remains lawful for the relation being claimed.

Something may resemble the matter in loose terms while still failing the specific relation being asserted. Material may therefore have apparent thematic proximity without lawful relation fit.

### 5.4 Scoped material

Scoped material is accepted material whose matter fit and relation fit have been lawfully bound to the present matter in a way that supports bounded applicability.

Scoped material is bounded matter-relevant handling, not a blanket rank or force claim.

## 6. Matter Coherence and Relation Coherence

Lawful scope depends on matter coherence and relation coherence.

Matter coherence requires that the material remain lawfully tied to the matter now at issue rather than merely resembling it, sitting near it, or appearing in the same preserved body.

Relation coherence requires that the claimed use remain lawful for the relation being asserted rather than drifting through convenience, retrieval habit, or ambient similarity.

This means something may be:

- real
- preserved
- readable
- proof-bearing
- intense
- locally adjacent

and still be out of scope for the present matter.

Adjacency, resemblance, atmospheric relevance, naming resemblance, directory proximity, and rhetorical force are not substitutes for lawful matter fit.

## 7. Scope, Presence, Threshold, and Standing

Scope and matter admissibility must remain distinct from later domains.

- scope and matter admissibility ask whether admitted material lawfully counts for the matter now at issue
- presence asks whether scoped material is now a relevant occurrence within that matter
- threshold asks whether enough has occurred within lawful scope for truth formation to become eligible
- standing belongs to the later regime in which truth and its preserved successors may stand

Scoped material does not settle those later domains.

Scoped is not present by default.
Scoped is not threshold.
Scoped is not standing.

If those distinctions are lost, bounded applicability begins to counterfeit later closure by mere proximity to the matter.

## 8. Scope and Matter Admissibility and Later Review

Scoped material may lawfully proceed toward later handling without thereby settling later review questions.

Once material is lawfully in scope, later review may still determine that:

- presence has not been established
- threshold has not been met
- the source relation is not lawful
- the transition posture is not lawful
- canon-facing or standing-facing pressure is not warranted

Scope and matter admissibility therefore open bounded matter-relevant handling. They do not close later review.

This is why scoped material can be real and still remain provisional with respect to source, transition, threshold, canon, and standing questions.

## 9. What Scope and Matter Admissibility Does Not Grant

Scope and matter admissibility do not themselves create:

- truth
- threshold sufficiency
- source legitimacy
- transition legitimacy
- final standing
- canon
- governance authorization
- consequence

They also do not convert atmosphere into lawful matter fit. Similarity, adjacency, naming resemblance, preservation, proof-bearing force, and readable residue may matter to why something is noticed, but they do not automatically make it lawfully in scope.

## 11. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming scope-and-matter-admissibility posture must not allow:

- accepted is not scoped
- scoped is not present by default
- scoped is not truth

- scoped is not standing
- scoped is not consequence
- preserved is not matter-fit by preservation alone
- resemblance is not lawful scope
- adjacency is not lawful scope
- rhetorical force is not lawful scope
- atmospheric relevance is not lawful matter fit
- bounded matter fit is not governance force

These rules are not stylistic. They are the minimum protections that keep bounded applicability from collapsing into later standing-state logic or into generic relevance atmosphere.
