# IAMMAI Contribution Capability Authority Governance Sovereignty Boundary

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the current system seam between contribution, capability, authority, governance, and sovereignty.

Its purpose is to make clear, in one bounded place:

- what each mode is
- what each mode is not
- where the non-convertible seams stand between them
- what successful contribution, usefulness, centrality, repeated success, and dependence still do not authorize
- how these modes may neighbor one another without silently upgrading into legitimacy, rightful rule, or sovereignty

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a general theory of power
- a governance rollout
- an institutional blueprint
- a runtime contract
- a vessel rollout
- an intelligence manifesto
- a social ontology
- a roadmap
- a README
- a project-management note
- a manifesto

This file is additive only. It does not replace repo-scale current-state, readability, rank, or lineage surfaces. It also does not replace existing admissibility, encounter, accession, implementation, continuity, runtime, vessel, participation, pilot, trace, or supervision surfaces already standing in the body.

## 4. Contribution Is Not Governance

Contribution is bounded participation in producing, shaping, supporting, formalizing, authorizing, rendering, transmitting, or otherwise materially relating to something in the body.

In the implementation-facing contribution-role line, contribution is already housed as typed participation relation to matter. It is stronger than generic authorship because it preserves how participation stood in relation to material.

Contribution does not authorize:

- governance force
- rightful rule
- currentness determination
- canon rank
- source status
- branch selection
- runtime admission
- broad touch permission
- sovereignty

Contribution may matter to provenance, preservation, review, or later lawful evaluation. It may be central. It may be necessary. It may be repeated. None of that makes contribution governance by default.

The seam is direct: contribution can support or inform a governed act, but it is not the governed act and does not become governance by being useful.

## 5. Capability Is Not Authority

Capability is real effective ability, competence, or consequence-bearing capacity.

The body now contains real examples of bounded capability: schema-shaped branches with checkers, invalid fixtures that fail as intended, and supervised pilot executions that were carried lawfully under review-before-admission discipline.

Capability does not authorize:

- lawful rank
- authority to decide
- authority to govern
- branch selection
- self-expansion
- runtime or API admission
- source, canon, or currentness status
- sovereignty

Capability may create a question for lawful placement. It may show that a surface, branch, or participant can do something real. It does not answer whether that ability may rule, decide, authorize, or expand.

The seam is direct: the ability to perform is not the authority to perform; the ability to help is not the right to govern; the ability to succeed repeatedly is not permission.

## 6. Authority Is Not Sovereignty

Authority is a lawful permission, standing, or bounded capacity to decide, authorize, preserve, validate, govern, or act in a specified way at a specified rank.

Authority is already different from contribution and capability because it depends on lawful admission, role, surface, and rank. It is not simply the most effective actor. It is not the contributor others depend on. It is not the tool or branch without which a task becomes harder.

Authority does not authorize:

- total jurisdiction
- final constitutional standing
- source status by role convenience
- canon rank by usefulness
- sovereignty
- expansion beyond the admitted scope
- self-promotion into broader rank

Authority may be real and still bounded. A validator may check without becoming governance. A checker may reject malformed fixtures without becoming an authority source. A currentness record may locate what governs without creating authority. A bounded decision surface may determine a posture without becoming sovereignty.

The seam is direct: authority, where it exists, remains scoped and admitted. It does not become sovereignty by being important.

## 7. Governance Is Not Sovereignty

Governance concerns lawful structuring of consequence, rule, decision, authorization, and protocol-significant action.

Governance is not merely influence, usefulness, dependence, competence, or repeated intervention. It is not generated automatically by the fact that a contribution was necessary or that a capability became operationally central.

Governance does not authorize:

- final lawful authority over the whole body
- constitutional source status
- canon identity
- native standing
- sovereignty
- expansion beyond the matter and rank lawfully governed
- authority to convert all successful operation into jurisdiction

Governance may be necessary for some runtime transitions or consequence-bearing acts. That still does not make governance sovereign by default. A governance action can be bounded, attributable, matter-scoped, and legitimacy-bound without becoming final rule over the body.

The seam is direct: governance may structure lawful consequence, but sovereignty is not produced by governance performance alone.

## 8. What Success Does Not Settle

Across the whole seam, success does not settle legitimacy.

The following do not by themselves authorize legitimacy, rightful rule, governance, sovereignty, source, canon, currentness, or broad touch permission:

- usefulness
- repeated success
- implementation maturity
- operational value
- centrality
- dependence
- familiarity
- branch maturity
- checker availability
- successful contribution
- successful pilot execution
- trace visibility
- supervised performance
- relation intensity

Repeated success is still not permission. Dependence is still not jurisdiction. Centrality is still not rightful rule. Capability is still not authority. Contribution is still not governance. Governance is still not sovereignty.

No successful derivative participant may promote itself into source, canon, currentness, governance, authority, or sovereignty by accumulation.

Across this seam, the following remain unauthorized unless separately admitted by lawful surfaces already standing elsewhere in the body:

- source
- canon
- currentness
- governance
- sovereignty
- rightful rule
- broad touch permission
- operational authority
- runtime, registry, API, or vessel operation

If a later claim says that usefulness has become authority, or capability has become governance, or governance has become sovereignty, the lawful question is not whether the claim feels plausible. The lawful question is what standing surface admitted that rank change. If no such surface stands, the jump does not stand.
