# IAMMAI Intake Admissibility

## 1. Purpose

This file defines intake admissibility as the bounded admissibility subdomain governing when candidate material may or may not lawfully enter structured protocol handling inside the IAMMAI body.

Its job is to clarify the lawful difference between mere encounter, candidate material, admitted intake, and the later domains that may follow after admission without letting those later domains collapse backward into intake itself.

It does not:

- rewrite the constitutional protocol
- create a new Acceptance section
- rewrite `runtime/VALIDATOR_CONTRACT.md`
- rewrite `admissibility/SOURCE_ADMISSIBILITY.md`
- define a canon admission procedure
- declare standing
- define a transition runtime flow map
- define a source-selection note
- act as a project-management note, README, or manifesto

This file is additive. It does not replace constitutional Acceptance or other ranked surfaces. It binds one admissibility domain more explicitly inside the body already standing.

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

The constitutional root for intake admissibility already exists.

The body now benefits from a clearer bounded file for intake admissibility because the repository has become dense enough that encounter, visibility, preserved presence, rhetorical force, and apparent relevance could otherwise begin to counterfeit lawful intake if the distinction remains under-specified outside the constitutional root.

That risk is now practical:

- candidate material may be encountered before lawful intake is made explicit
- preserved material may appear serious or intense without thereby becoming admissible for handling
- visible lineage may appear protocol-relevant without yet being lawfully admitted
- apparent relevance may be mistaken for admissible entry

This file exists to reduce that risk by bounded clarification, not by rewriting the constitutional source that already carries the root posture.

## 3. Existing Root in the Body

The existing root for intake admissibility is constitutional.

At constitutional rank, `protocol/IAMMAI_Public_Constitutional_Protocol_v1.0.pdf`, `protocol/IAMMAI_Constitutional_Integrity_Protocol_v1.0.1.md`, and `protocol/canon.json` already bind the acceptance-facing admissible-intake posture.

Those surfaces already state that:

- the canonical phase order includes `signal → acceptance → scope → presence → threshold → truth → continuity → decision → consequence`
- Acceptance belongs to the preparation regime, not the standing-state regime
- Acceptance is the phase by which candidate signal becomes admitted for structured protocol handling
- Acceptance owns admissible intake, not truth
- accepted material may still later fail scope, threshold, or other later review

`protocol/canon.json` also already names the job of Acceptance as `admissible_intake` and marks it as non-standing-state.

This file therefore binds and clarifies an existing constitutional root. It does not create a new one.

## 4. What Intake Admissibility Answers

Intake admissibility answers the bounded question:

**May this candidate material lawfully enter structured protocol handling at all?**

This is an intake question.

It is not yet:

- a truth question
- a standing question
- a consequence question
- a source-selection question
- a transition-legitimacy question

Intake admissibility authorizes handling posture in bounded form. It does not by itself decide what later phases will conclude.

## 5. Encounter, Candidate Material, and Admitted Intake

These must remain distinct.

### 5.1 Mere encounter

Mere encounter is contact, appearance, or availability before lawful intake has been granted.

Encounter may be real, visible, preserved, intense, rhetorically strong, or apparently relevant. None of those facts alone make it admitted intake.

### 5.2 Candidate material

Candidate material is material available to the system as signal for possible handling.

Candidate material is already more specific than mere encounter, but it is still not admitted intake by default. Candidate status means the material is in view for possible protocol handling, not that lawful intake has already occurred.

### 5.3 Admitted intake

Admitted intake is the bounded handling status produced when candidate material passes the acceptance-facing intake question.

Admitted intake means the material may lawfully enter structured protocol handling.

It does not mean the material has gained:

- truth
- standing
- scope fit
- threshold satisfaction
- consequence

Admitted intake is therefore a bounded handling status, not a blanket rank or force claim.

## 6. Intake, Scope, Threshold, and Standing

Intake admissibility must remain distinct from later domains.

- intake admissibility asks whether candidate material may enter structured handling at all
- scope and matter admissibility ask whether admitted material lawfully fits the matter or relation now at issue
- threshold asks whether materially relevant content has reached eligibility for truth formation
- standing belongs to the later regime in which truth and its preserved successors may stand

Admitted intake does not settle those later domains.

Accepted is not scoped.
Accepted is not threshold.
Accepted is not standing.

If those distinctions are lost, the preparation regime begins to counterfeit standing by accumulation alone.

## 7. Intake Admissibility and Later Review

Admitted intake may lawfully proceed toward later handling without thereby settling later review questions.

Once material is lawfully admitted for structured handling, later review may still determine that:

- the matter fit is not lawful
- the source relation is not lawful
- the transition posture is not lawful
- standing-facing or canon-facing pressure is not warranted
- threshold has not been met

Intake admissibility therefore opens bounded handling. It does not close later review.

This is why admitted intake can be real and still remain provisional with respect to later domains.

## 8. What Intake Admissibility Does Not Grant

Intake admissibility does not itself create:

- truth
- scope fit
- source legitimacy
- transition legitimacy
- final standing
- canon
- governance authorization
- consequence

It also does not convert atmosphere into law. Visibility, urgency, intensity, preserved presence, and rhetorical force may matter as part of why something is encountered or noticed, but they do not automatically create lawful intake.

## 10. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming intake-admissibility posture must not allow:

- encountered is not accepted
- visible is not intake-admissible by default
- preserved is not admitted intake by preservation alone
- intensity is not lawful intake
- rhetorical force is not lawful intake
- accepted is not scoped
- accepted is not truth
- accepted is not threshold
- accepted is not standing
- accepted is not consequence
- admitted handling is not governance force

These rules are not stylistic. They are the minimum protections that keep preparation from collapsing into later standing-state logic.

## 11. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines intake admissibility only.

Later files may elaborate other admissibility domains. This file does not attempt to solve source admissibility, transition admissibility, canon-facing admission, or standing review in one step.

It exists to make lawful intake more explicit inside the current IAMMAI body without rewriting constitutional Acceptance or collapsing preparation into standing, truth, or consequence.
