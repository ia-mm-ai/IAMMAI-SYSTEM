# IAMMAI Lawful Transfer Seam v0

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded continuity note for lawful transfer across system boundaries in IAMMAI.

Its job is to name the seam condition under which something may cross from one boundary to another without collapsing source canon, lineage, or jurisdiction.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a runtime contract
- a schema
- a full cross-system stack
- a source-release note
- a destination-ingress note
- a generic interoperability theory

It is a bounded continuity-facing document.

## 2. First Principle

The lawful transfer seam is subordinate to constitutional invariant.

Transfer must not collapse lineage, jurisdiction, or canonical distinction.  
Not everything may cross.  
Crossing may change jurisdiction without erasing origin.

In IAMMAI terms, transfer is not free movement. It is a bounded crossing whose lawfulness depends on keeping origin relation explicit while boundary and jurisdiction change.

## 3. Why a Lawful Transfer Seam Is Needed

Continuity already exists within bounded runs.

Once a preserved artifact, relation, or reference is carried across one system boundary toward another, crossing needs explicit seam logic rather than an assumption that continuity simply survives unchanged.

Without a seam, handoff risks:

- false continuity
- broken lineage
- jurisdiction confusion

## 4. What a Lawful Transfer Seam Is

A lawful transfer seam is a bounded crossing condition between one system boundary and another.

It preserves source relation.  
It preserves what remains canonical at origin.  
It names what may be carried across without making destination treat it as native by default.

## 5. What May Cross

At a high level, the following may cross where lawful and bounded:

- bounded references
- continuity relations
- carried artifacts where lawful
- explicit transfer metadata

## 6. What Must Not Cross

At a high level, the following must not cross:

- silent authority transfer
- erased origin
- collapsed canonical versus derivative distinction
- fabricated continuity
- untyped jurisdiction shift

## 7. What Must Remain Preserved

The seam must preserve:

- source legibility
- type legibility
- predecessor or origin relation where applicable
- temporal order
- traceability
- reconstructability

## 8. Jurisdiction Change at Crossing

Source canon remains at source.

Destination receipt does not imply native standing.  
Carried presence does not equal native origin.  
Transfer changes boundary and jurisdiction without erasing source relation.

## 9. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming lawful transfer seam must not allow:

- transfer without source legibility
- transfer that erases predecessor or origin
- authority crossing by convenience
- derivative surface silently promoted to canonical standing
- fabricated continuity to smooth crossing
- silent jurisdiction inheritance

## 10. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines only the seam.

Release and ingress may be articulated later elsewhere. This file does not replace the constitutional core, continuity layer, registry, or runtime layers.
