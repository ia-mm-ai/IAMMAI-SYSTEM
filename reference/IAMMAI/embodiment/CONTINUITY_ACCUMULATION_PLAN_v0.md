# IAMMAI Continuity Accumulation Plan v0

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded Level 5 continuity accumulation plan for IAMMAI.

Its job is to explain what continuity accumulation means in the current body, why this phase comes after sequential pressure, what kinds of runs should be accumulated, what must remain invariant while continuity accumulates, what should be observed across the accumulated successful turns, and what stop conditions should prevent reckless escalation or fake continuity.

It does not yet try to define:

- code
- a graph model
- a visualization plan
- a public continuity surface
- a richer continuity engine
- a sovereign historical layer

It is a bounded embodiment, continuity, and dynamics bridge document.

## 2. First Principle

Continuity accumulation must remain subordinate to canonical preservation.

Accumulation concerns repeated successful lawful turns, not narrative inflation.  
More turns do not automatically equal more understanding.  
Visible non-passage remains important, but Level 5 itself should focus on successful bounded runs.

In IAMMAI terms, continuity accumulation is not a claim that thicker history makes the body truer by itself. It is the bounded observation of whether continuity remains honest when more successful continuity-bearing turns begin to exist side by side.

## 3. Why Continuity Accumulation Is Needed Now

Continuity is already present but still thin.

The current body already emits continuity turns for successful lawful runs, but that layer has so far remained close to first introduction. A thin continuity layer can be conceptually sound while still not yet having enough successful history to show whether it stays bounded when the number of continuity-bearing turns increases.

Repeated successful runs now make accumulation relevant.

This phase comes after mixed sequential pressure because the body has already shown that it can carry valid, invalid, and edge-near runs under cumulative sequence pressure. The next narrower question is not whether mixed pressure exists, but whether successful continuity-bearing turns remain truthful when they accumulate into a thicker local history.

Continuity must therefore be observed as a growing layer, not merely assumed.

## 4. What Remains Invariant During Continuity Accumulation

The following must remain the same across the accumulation phase:

- same matter class remains bounded local note
- same runtime role family remains in play
- same canonical artifact family remains in play
- same local execution boundary remains in force
- same canonical versus derivative separation remains in force
- same continuity emission posture remains in force
- same non-public local-first posture remains in force

Continuity accumulation may increase the number of successful continuity-bearing turns. It may not widen the slice class, change the artifact family, broaden access, or convert continuity into a richer architecture by implication.

## 5. Allowed Accumulation Material

The Level 5 accumulation phase may include:

- successful bounded note runs
- successful borderline-valid runs
- already proven slice-class successes

Failed and non-passing runs remain important history and must remain visible in the broader body. They are not erased by this phase.

Level 5 itself should focus on successful continuity-bearing turns because the narrow question here is whether continuity remains truthful, append-oriented, and artifact-linked when more successful turns accumulate.

This is a safe first batch because it stays modest, fully local, and entirely inside the already-proven slice class while spanning both ordinary successful notes and thinner edge-successful notes. It is thick enough to make accumulation visible without implying that continuity now needs richer geometry or broader embodiment.

## 7. What To Observe Across Accumulated Turns

Across the batch, the following should be watched:

- continuity turns remain present for every successful run
- continuity turns remain artifact-linked and non-sovereign
- no continuity turn outruns preserved canonical artifacts
- continuity remains append-oriented
- no pressure toward fake predecessor relations
- no softening of canonical versus derivative boundary
- no hidden widening of slice identity

The key question is not whether there are simply more continuity artifacts. The key question is whether those additional continuity turns remain as honest as the first ones when the successful history becomes thicker.

## 8. What Would Count as a Repeating Continuity Seam

Meaningful continuity seams would include patterns such as:

- continuity turns becoming too detached from actual artifact families
- continuity beginning to behave like summary fiction
- repeated ambiguity around predecessor or successor relation
- continuity records appearing thicker than the preserved runs can honestly support
- continuity accumulation pressuring the body toward premature richer geometry

These would count as seams because they show repeated pressure on the bounded continuity posture rather than one isolated awkward record or one merely unfamiliar batch size.

## 9. Stop Conditions

The accumulation phase should stop or pause if any of the following begin to hold:

- continuity begins to outrun preserved artifacts
- canonical versus derivative separation weakens
- repeated ambiguity appears in the same continuity seam
- slice identity begins to widen
- pressure arises to invent predecessor relations that are not yet lawfully grounded

The phase should also stop if accumulation begins to imply that more runs by themselves justify broader meaning, richer continuity geometry, or more authoritative continuity claims than the preserved canonical records can actually support.

## 10. What This Phase Is Trying To Prove

This phase is trying to show:

- whether continuity remains truthful and bounded across a thicker successful history
- whether continuity may stay in v0 form longer
- whether repeated seams now justify deeper continuity work later

If continuity remains append-oriented, artifact-linked, and subordinate to preserved runs across the batch, that supports keeping continuity in its current bounded form longer.

If repeated continuity seams emerge, especially in the same seam family, that would justify later bounded continuity refinement rather than assuming that v0 continuity can simply thicken indefinitely without additional work.

## 11. Relation to Future Work

If continuity accumulation holds, checkpoint and reassess.

If repeated seams emerge, consider bounded continuity refinement later.

Drift and stability should be read through this phase because a thicker successful continuity history matters for later claims about whether the body holds coherently over time without narrative inflation or semantic softening.

Adaptation remains out of play for now. This phase is not a permission surface for broader change. It is a bounded way to see whether continuity can remain truthful as successful local history thickens.

This document defines the first bounded Level 5 continuity accumulation plan of IAMMAI as the modest local accumulation of successful continuity-bearing turns within the same bounded local note slice so continuity can be observed across a thicker successful history without widening the body.

Its purpose is to test whether continuity remains append-oriented, artifact-linked, and subordinate to canonical preservation as successful runs accumulate, or whether repeated seams now begin to justify later bounded continuity refinement.
