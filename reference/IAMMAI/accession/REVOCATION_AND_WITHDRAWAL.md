# IAMMAI Revocation and Withdrawal

## 1. Purpose

This file defines Revocation and Withdrawal as the accession sublayer where one admitted or bound vessel slice may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked without erasing lineage or counterfeiting non-history.

Its job is to make explicit how the body lawfully exits, reduces, pauses, or terminates an accession path after candidacy, shadow, proof, admission, or bound vessel mode have already occurred in bounded form.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a sovereign authority layer
- a Contact Law file
- an eligibility file
- a shadow-mode file
- an accession proof file
- an admission decision
- a bound vessel contract
- a migration strategy memo
- a product offboarding handbook
- a roadmap
- a project-management surface

This file is additive only. It does not replace the constitutional core, the admissibility surfaces, the continuity surfaces, the standing-facing review surfaces, the vessel-family surfaces, [`accession/CONTACT_LAW.md`](./CONTACT_LAW.md), [`accession/ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md`](./ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md), [`accession/SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md`](./SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md), [`accession/ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md`](./ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md), [`accession/ADMISSION_DECISION.md`](./ADMISSION_DECISION.md), [`accession/BOUND_VESSEL_MODE.md`](./BOUND_VESSEL_MODE.md), or the master map in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

Once bounded accession can proceed through admission and vessel mode, the body also needs a lawful exit and reduction posture.

Without Revocation and Withdrawal, admission risks becoming sticky privilege, bound vessel mode risks becoming hidden dependency, and earlier bounded accession stages risk being treated as though they can only move forward.

Without lineage preservation, exit also risks becoming historical falsification. The body must be able to stop, narrow, or unwind relation without pretending that earlier lawful stages never happened.

This file exists to make lawful exit legible without overbuilding it.

## 3. Existing Position in the Body

Revocation and Withdrawal are the final mapped sublayer already named in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

They follow Contact Law, Accession Eligibility, Shadow Slice Mode, Accession Proof Slice, Admission Decision, and Bound Vessel Mode.

This file therefore binds a mapped sublayer rather than inventing a new family. It stands at the point where one accession path may be lawfully reduced, paused, exited, or terminated without non-history theater.

## 4. What Revocation and Withdrawal Answer

Revocation and Withdrawal answer the bounded question:

**Under what lawful conditions may one accession path be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked without falsifying lineage?**

This is a post-accession-path governance question only.

It asks how one bounded slice may be reduced, stopped, or exited after bounded accession handling has already occurred. It does not ask for constitutional damnation, does not create standing, does not create canon, and does not create closure.

## 5. Revocation Is Not Erasure

The following distinctions must remain explicit.

- Narrowing is not erasure.
- Pause is not erasure.
- Withdrawal is not retroactive non-existence.
- Revocation is not retroactive non-existence.
- Revocation is not historical wipe.

Revocation means that a bounded relation, privilege, or operational posture is explicitly withdrawn or terminated.

Withdrawal means that a bounded accession path is explicitly exited rather than silently allowed to linger.

Neither one means that proof history, admission history, vessel history, or prior bounded handling never occurred.

Revocation and Withdrawal therefore preserve history while changing present relation.

## 6. What Revocation and Withdrawal Do

Revocation and Withdrawal legitimately do the following in bounded form.

### Lawful Reduction Of Scope

They may reduce the scope of an accession relation where the slice should continue only under narrower conditions.

### Lawful Pause

They may pause a bounded accession relation where continued handling or operation is not presently lawful, not presently needed, or not presently clear enough to continue.

### Lawful Termination Of A Bounded Relation

They may explicitly stop a candidate relation, shadow relation, proof posture, admitted relation, or bound vessel relation.

### Visible Preservation Of What Occurred

They preserve visible record of what occurred. Candidate history, shadow history, proof history, admission history, and vessel history remain lineage-visible even where present relation is reduced or terminated.

### Refusal Of Silent Cleanup

They refuse silent cleanup, prestige laundering, and embarrassment-driven rewrite. The body does not regain innocence by pretending that prior bounded relation never happened.

### Maintained Provenance And Jurisdiction Distinction

They preserve source legibility and jurisdiction distinction even when the relation is narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked.

These are infrastructural exit functions. They are not punishments and not dramas.

## 7. What Revocation and Withdrawal Do Not Do

Revocation and Withdrawal do not lawfully do the following.

- They do not erase proof history.
- They do not erase admission history.
- They do not erase vessel history.
- They do not erase source relation.
- They do not condemn a whole external system because one slice was reduced or revoked.
- They do not create punishment theater.
- They do not create prestige retaliation.
- They do not become founder revenge.

Founder ownership, usefulness, familiarity, urgency, or proximity do not create hidden immunity from revocation, and they do not create hidden privilege to withdraw history from view.

Exit is therefore non-mythic and lineage-preserving. It changes present relation without falsifying what occurred.

## 8. Stage-Specific Revocation and Withdrawal

Revocation and Withdrawal may apply at different bounded stages. Later-stage reduction does not rewrite earlier-stage reality.

### Candidate Level

At candidate level, a slice may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked as a candidate. This does not erase the fact that candidacy once stood in bounded form.

### Shadow Level

At shadow level, shadow may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked. This does not erase the fact that one slice was lawfully mirrored for bounded inspection.

### Proof Level

At proof level, proof handling may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked. This does not erase the fact that the slice entered a proof posture or that proof-visible failures or limits became part of lineage.

### Admission Level

At admission level, admitted relation may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked. This does not erase the fact that bounded admission once stood.

### Bound Vessel Mode Level

At bound vessel mode level, vessel scope may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked. This does not erase the fact that bounded vessel operation once stood under explicit law.

Stage-specific exit therefore governs present posture, not retroactive non-history.

## 9. Lawful Outcomes

Revocation and Withdrawal lawfully permit bounded accession outcomes only.

### Narrow

The slice may remain in relation, but under smaller scope, stricter conditions, or reduced operational privilege.

### Pause

The slice may remain historically legible while current relation is suspended.

### Withdraw

The slice may exit an accession path explicitly, without requiring condemnation or falsification.

### Revoke

The slice may have a previously granted bounded relation or bounded operational posture explicitly terminated.

### Preserve History

Whatever the outcome, lineage remains visible. Source legibility, proof history, admission history, and vessel history do not disappear merely because the present relation changed.

These are bounded accession outcomes only. They do not create standing, canon, or closure on the negative side.

## 10. What Revocation and Withdrawal Do Not Grant

Revocation and Withdrawal do not themselves create:

- standing
- canon
- closure
- historical innocence
- whole-system invalidation

They also do not grant:

- founder privilege
- usefulness privilege
- urgency privilege
- familiarity privilege
- proximity privilege

Even when negative, Revocation and Withdrawal remain bounded accession postures only.
