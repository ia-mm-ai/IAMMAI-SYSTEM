# IAMMAI Accession Map

## 1. Purpose

This file provides a compact entry surface for the first complete Accession family.

Its job is to make the family easy to enter, read, and use as one coherent slice-first external-relation stack without rewriting the standing accession files.

It does not:

- replace [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md)
- re-explain the whole accession doctrine in full
- flatten the sublayers into one undifferentiated block
- act as a roadmap, migration plan, or product integration memo
- invent new accession sublayers beyond what already stands

This file is additive only. It does not replace the standing accession files.

## 2. Why This Map Is Needed Now

The first accession family now stands in full first-pass form.

The body therefore benefits from one compact entry surface so the family can be read coherently as one bounded stack rather than only as separate files discovered piecemeal.

This file exists to orient, not to re-litigate the doctrine.

## 3. Family Shape

The Accession family has two sublayers.

- `Contact Law`: governs how the outside may lawfully approach the body at all.
- `Accession Law`: governs how one bounded external slice may proceed, if lawful, through eligibility, shadow, proof, admission, bound vessel mode, and revocation or withdrawal.

The accession sequence is:

1. contact
2. eligibility
3. shadow
4. proof
5. admission
6. bound vessel mode
7. revocation / withdrawal

## 4. File-by-File Map

- [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md): defines the master map of lawful external relation and identifies Contact Law and Accession Law as the two accession sublayers.
- [`accession/CONTACT_LAW.md`](./CONTACT_LAW.md): defines the first threshold at which outside approach may be observed, refused, treated as out of scope, or treated as insufficiently grounded without being promoted into candidacy.
- [`accession/ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md`](./ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md): defines the candidate threshold at which one bounded external slice may or may not become an accession candidate after contact and before shadow.
- [`accession/SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md`](./SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md): defines the non-native mirroring posture in which one eligible slice may be inspected and compared under body law without admission or vesselhood.
- [`accession/ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md`](./ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md): defines the bounded proof posture in which one shadowed slice may or may not prove that it can stand under body law without merger or hidden authority carryover.
- [`accession/ADMISSION_DECISION.md`](./ADMISSION_DECISION.md): defines the explicit yes / no / not-yet decision posture in which one proved slice may or may not enter admitted bounded relation.
- [`accession/BOUND_VESSEL_MODE.md`](./BOUND_VESSEL_MODE.md): defines the separately granted operational posture in which one admitted slice may function as a derivative, source-limited, non-sovereign vessel within explicit scope.
- [`accession/REVOCATION_AND_WITHDRAWAL.md`](./REVOCATION_AND_WITHDRAWAL.md): defines the bounded exit and reduction posture in which one accession path may be narrowed, paused, withdrawn, or revoked without erasing lineage.

## 5. Core Invariants

The following anti-collapse rules hold across the family.

- contact is not accession
- eligibility is not admission
- shadow is not vesselhood
- proof is not closure
- admission is slice-specific
- bound vessel mode is not native source
- revocation is not erasure
- one admitted slice is not whole-system inheritance
- source legibility and jurisdiction distinction must remain intact throughout
- founder ownership, usefulness, familiarity, urgency, and proximity do not create hidden accession privilege

This first-pass family is now complete as a mapped stack.

That does not imply closure of the wider body, closure of future accession cases, or automatic readiness of any outside system for accession.
