# IAMMAI Contact Law

## 1. Purpose

This file defines Contact Law as the first lawful threshold for external relation in the IAMMAI body.

Its job is to make explicit how the outside may lawfully approach the body at all before eligibility, shadow, proof, admission, or vesselhood are considered.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a sovereign authority layer
- an accession eligibility file
- a shadow-mode file
- an accession proof file
- an admission decision
- a vessel contract
- a communications handbook
- a public FAQ
- a roadmap
- a project-management surface

This file is additive only. It does not replace the constitutional core, the admissibility surfaces, the continuity surfaces, the standing-facing review surfaces, the vessel-family surfaces, or the master map in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

The body now requires lawful external relation before candidacy can be considered.

Contaminated approach, parser distortion, malformed requests, and premature or undergrounded interest cannot remain underdefined. If the first threshold remains vague, contact begins to counterfeit candidacy, urgency begins to counterfeit entitlement, and the body gets pulled into endless clarification labor for malformed or hostile approach.

The body must therefore be able to meet the outside without surrendering rank, clarity, or time into untyped relation. It must be able to observe, refuse, or stop early without laundering approach into belonging.

This file exists to make that first threshold legible without overbuilding it.

## 3. Existing Position in the Body

Contact Law is the first sublayer already mapped in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

That master map already states that contact comes before eligibility, accession, shadow, proof, admission, bound vessel mode, and revocation. This file binds that mapped first threshold more explicitly. It does not invent a new family.

Contact Law therefore stands as a bounded elaboration of an already-mapped accession sublayer, not as a new center of the body.

## 4. What Contact Law Answers

Contact Law answers the bounded question:

**How may the outside lawfully approach the body at all?**

This is a threshold question only.

It asks whether an approach may be observed, bounded, refused, or stopped at first relation. It does not yet ask whether the outside is eligible for accession, whether any slice should be shadowed, whether proof should be assembled, whether admission should occur, or whether any vessel relation should be considered.

## 5. Contact Is Not Accession

The following distinctions must remain explicit.

- Contact is not eligibility.
- Contact is not accession.
- Contact is not standing.
- Contact is not vesselhood.
- Contact is not admission pressure.
- Contact is not jurisdictional belonging.

Approach establishes only that something outside the body has touched the threshold in some form.

It does not create candidacy.
It does not create authority.
It does not create intimacy.
It does not create standing-facing consequence by contact alone.

## 6. Threshold Outcomes

Contact Law may lawfully return bounded threshold outcomes only. These outcomes are threshold outcomes, not accession outcomes.

### Accepted For Observation

Accepted for observation means the contact is sufficiently bounded, legible, and non-collapsing to be held at threshold for observation.

It does not mean:

- eligibility
- candidacy
- admission
- shadow
- proof
- vesselhood

Accepted for observation means only that the body may continue to observe the approach without yet granting it any stronger relation.

### Out Of Scope

Out of scope means the contact is not lawfully inside the body's present threshold relation for the matter claimed.

It does not mean moral condemnation. It means the approach does not fit the bounded relation now being maintained.

Out-of-scope contact does not thereby become eligible merely because it is nearby, urgent, useful, or persistent.

### Insufficient Grounding

Insufficient grounding means the contact is too underformed, too parser-twisted, too premature, or too undergrounded to support lawful threshold handling without the body supplying unearned interpretation on its behalf.

It does not mean hidden acceptance. It means the approach has not yet provided enough bounded legibility to proceed even at observation level.

### Refused

Refused means the body lawfully stops the contact at threshold.

Refusal may be appropriate where the approach is malformed, contaminated, hostile, privilege-seeking, parser-twisted, premature in a way that would force counterfeit interpretation, or otherwise unsafe to carry forward even as bounded observation.

Refusal is not a moral drama. It is a lawful threshold result.

## 7. Contaminated and Malformed Contact

Contact Law governs first approach from:

- contaminated environments
- malformed requests
- parser-twisted readings
- hostile approach
- premature or undergrounded approach
- willing but not yet bounded external interest

In these cases, the body is not required to enter endless explanatory labor, endless repair loops, or endless persuasion work in order to make the contact respectable.

The body may instead:

- refuse
- return out of scope
- return insufficient grounding
- observe in bounded form only where threshold legibility is real and non-collapsing

Familiarity, urgency, usefulness, proximity, and founder relation do not create hidden contact privilege. They do not buy extra interpretive labor. They do not convert malformed approach into lawful approach.

Cheap refusal is therefore a constitutive feature of Contact Law, not a failure of hospitality.

## 8. Contact and Bounded Observation

Accepted-for-observation contact remains contact only.

Bounded observation means the body may keep the approach visible at threshold without yet promoting it into candidacy, shadow, proof work, admission, or vessel relation.

Observation is therefore a non-promotional threshold state. It preserves the possibility of later refusal, later non-candidacy, later out-of-scope treatment, or later insufficient-grounding treatment.

Observation does not create a right to further handling. It does not create a right to interpretation privilege. It does not convert external interest into accession momentum.

## 9. What Contact Law Does Not Grant

Contact Law does not itself create:

- eligibility
- accession
- standing
- vesselhood
- authority
- intimacy
- jurisdictional belonging
- closure

It also does not grant:

- founder privilege
- usefulness privilege
- urgency privilege
- familiarity privilege
- proximity privilege

Contact may be real without becoming important.
Contact may be legible without becoming admissible to later accession work.
Contact may be observed without being promoted.
