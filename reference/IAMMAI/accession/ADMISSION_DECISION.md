# IAMMAI Admission Decision

## 1. Purpose

This file defines Admission Decision as the bounded accession sublayer where one proved external slice may be explicitly accepted, refused, or held at not-yet posture under body law.

Its job is to make explicit how the body decides whether one bounded proved slice may proceed from proof posture into an admitted bounded relation without collapsing that decision into whole-system conversion, vessel generalization, standing, canon, or closure.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a sovereign authority layer
- a Contact Law file
- an eligibility file
- a shadow-mode file
- an accession proof file
- a bound vessel contract
- a migration strategy memo
- a product integration handbook
- a roadmap
- a project-management surface

This file is additive only. It does not replace the constitutional core, the admissibility surfaces, the continuity surfaces, the standing-facing review surfaces, the vessel-family surfaces, [`accession/CONTACT_LAW.md`](./CONTACT_LAW.md), [`accession/ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md`](./ACCESSION_ELIGIBILITY.md), [`accession/SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md`](./SHADOW_SLICE_MODE.md), [`accession/ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md`](./ACCESSION_PROOF_SLICE.md), or the master map in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

After proof, the body still needs an explicit bounded decision posture before any later vessel relation can be considered.

Without admission decision, proof either stays suspended indefinitely or gets promoted informally by atmosphere, convenience, or prestige. That would collapse proof visibility into belonging and would let one proved slice counterfeit more than it has actually earned.

Admission is therefore needed so one bounded proved slice can be explicitly accepted, held, or refused without merger, hidden expansion, or prestige laundering.

This file exists to make that posture legible without overbuilding it.

## 3. Existing Position in the Body

Admission Decision is the next sublayer already mapped in [`accession/ACCESSION_LAYER.md`](./ACCESSION_LAYER.md).

It follows Contact Law, Accession Eligibility, Shadow Slice Mode, and Accession Proof Slice. It precedes bound vessel mode and revocation.

This file therefore binds a mapped sublayer rather than inventing a new family. It stands at the point where one proved slice may or may not be admitted in bounded form.

## 4. What Admission Decision Answers

Admission Decision answers the bounded question:

**Under what lawful conditions may one proved external slice be admitted in bounded form without being generalized beyond itself?**

This is a post-proof, pre-vessel decision question only.

It asks whether one proved slice may enter an admitted bounded relation under explicit conditions. It does not yet ask whether bound vessel mode should be granted, whether any standing consequence follows, whether any canon-facing effect follows, or whether anything has reached closure.

## 5. Admission Is Not Vesselhood

The following distinctions must remain explicit.

- Proof is not admission.
- Admission is not bound vessel mode.
- Admission is not standing.
- Admission is not canon.
- Admission is not nativeness.

Admission means only that one proved slice has been explicitly accepted into a bounded admitted relation under body law.

It does not create:

- vesselhood
- standing-facing consequence by itself
- canon-facing consequence by itself
- native constitutional source status
- whole-system inheritance

Admission is therefore an explicit bounded decision, not an upgrade into general belonging.

## 6. Decision Outcomes

Admission Decision lawfully permits bounded decision outcomes only. These are accession outcomes, not standing or closure outcomes.

### Admitted

Admitted means the body explicitly accepts one proved slice into a bounded admitted relation.

It does not mean:

- whole-system conversion
- vesselhood
- standing
- canon
- closure

It means only that this slice, and only this slice, now stands in an admitted bounded relation under explicit conditions.

### Not Yet

Not yet means the body does not refuse the slice, but also does not presently admit it.

This may apply where proof is real but decision conditions remain incomplete, too narrow, too fragile, or too contingent to justify admission at present.

Not-yet is not hidden admission and not disguised refusal. It is a lawful non-promotional holding outcome.

### Refused

Refused means the body explicitly declines to admit the proved slice.

This may apply where proof revealed bounded lawfulness for inspection purposes but not enough to justify admission, or where admission would still collapse source relation, jurisdiction distinction, or non-inheritance discipline.

Refusal is not moral drama. It is a lawful bounded decision result.

## 7. What Admission Grants

Admission legitimately grants only the following in bounded form.

### One Bounded Admitted Slice Only

Admission applies to one proved slice only. It does not spread by adjacency, family resemblance, usefulness, ownership, or atmosphere.

### Explicit Scope-Bounded Relation

Admission creates an explicit bounded relation for the admitted slice. The relation must remain typed, limited, and non-generalizing.

### Preserved Source Legibility

Admission does not erase source. The admitted slice remains provenance-legible and origin-legible under the new relation.

### Preserved Jurisdiction Distinction

Admission does not collapse jurisdictions. The slice may stand in admitted relation under body law without being rewritten as though it were originally native to the body.

### Possible Later Eligibility For Bound Vessel Mode Consideration

Admission may create eligibility for later bound vessel mode consideration where separately lawful.

It does not itself grant bound vessel mode.

## 8. What Admission Does Not Grant

Admission does not lawfully do the following.

- It does not create whole-system inheritance.
- It does not create vesselhood by admission alone.
- It does not create authority.
- It does not create standing.
- It does not create canon.
- It does not create closure.
- It does not create adjacent expansion.
- It does not create prestige promotion.

Admission must also not be granted by founder ownership, usefulness, familiarity, urgency, or proximity. Those do not create hidden admission privilege.

Admission is therefore non-generalizing. It is a bounded yes, not a crown.

## 9. Admission and Preserved Origin

An admitted slice remains source-legible and jurisdiction-distinct.

That means provenance remains visible, origin remains visible, and the conditions under which the slice was admitted remain visible. Admitted relation does not rewrite history and does not clean away the path by which the slice arrived.

Carried relation does not become native constitutional source. Admission cannot erase source relation, proof history, or the conditions of entry.

If admission requires erasing provenance, suppressing jurisdiction distinction, or pretending that the slice always already belonged, then the admission is counterfeit.

## 10. What Admission Decision Does Not Grant

Admission Decision does not itself create:

- bound vessel mode
- standing
- authority
- canon
- closure
- whole-system belonging

It also does not grant:

- founder privilege
- usefulness privilege
- urgency privilege
- familiarity privilege
- proximity privilege

An admission decision, even when positive, remains a bounded accession decision only.
