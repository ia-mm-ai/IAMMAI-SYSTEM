# IAMMAI v1 Authority and Inheritance Map

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the high-level repo authority and inheritance posture between the frozen root `v0` source-side body and the `v1/` derivation line.

Its job is to distinguish what remains authoritative from source-side, what remains visible as evidence and lineage only, and what class of surfaces may later need explicit re-derivation inside `v1/`.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a runtime contract
- a schema map
- a full re-derivation plan
- a README

It is a bounded repo-governance map.

## 2. First Principle

Authority and inheritance do not pass by implication.

Source-side authority remains source-side unless explicitly retained.  
Evidence and lineage may remain visible without remaining authoritative.  
Re-derivation is not denial of source; it is explicit downstream restatement.

## 3. Why This Map Is Needed

Without this map, root and `v1/` can be read flatly.

Flat reading produces false contradiction, false inheritance, or false supersession. A later derivation line can appear to inherit authority merely by adjacency, while historically real source-side material can appear either falsely displaced or falsely equal in rank to later downstream restatement.

This map exists to keep authority ranking explicit.

## 4. Source-Side Authoritative Inheritance

The following classes are expected to remain authoritative from root unless later explicitly superseded:

- constitutional source artifacts and their governing priority
- core architectural distinctions that carry constitutional law into body form without changing it
- stable implementation doctrine where the law itself has not changed and where later derivation remains a restatement rather than a correction

These classes remain source-side authoritative because they define or carry the governing law, the main anti-collapse distinctions, and the minimum typed body posture from which later derivation must proceed.

## 5. Source-Side Evidence and Lineage Only

The following classes remain visible and important as source evidence and lineage, but should not automatically function as ongoing `v1` authority:

- emitted proof artifacts
- seam and lab traces
- unstable enforcement surfaces
- historically specific explanatory surfaces overtaken by later body reality

These remain important because they show what formed, what drifted, what held, and what required later derivation. They do not automatically stand as ongoing downstream authority merely because they remain present in the root tree.

## 6. Likely Re-Derivation Surfaces

The following classes are likely to require explicit `v1` re-derivation:

- artifact boundary and artifact law
- schemas where doctrine and emitted shape drifted
- emitter and enforcement surfaces
- fixture classification
- explanatory surfaces whose posture no longer matches the body

These are re-derivation candidates because they sit closest to drift, mismatch, enforcement instability, or explanatory posture that may no longer cleanly fit the later derivation line.

## 7. Inheritance Law

The governing inheritance rules are:

- no authority by adjacency
- no inheritance by vibe
- no silent supersession
- no flat reading of root and `v1/` as equal-rank surfaces
- retention, supersession, and re-derivation must each be explicit

## 8. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines repo-level authority and inheritance posture only.

Later files may decide artifact boundaries and concrete re-derivation paths. This map exists so `v1/` can inherit lawfully without falsifying `v0`.
