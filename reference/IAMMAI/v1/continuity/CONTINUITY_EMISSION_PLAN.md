# IAMMAI v1 Continuity Emission Plan

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the bounded v1 continuity-specific emission plan.

Its job is to state what the first v1 continuity emission round is supposed to emit, in what lawful order, and under what layer distinctions, without becoming code and without silently re-importing the old v0 run-centric posture.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a continuity schema
- a continuity validator implementation note
- a continuity emitter patch
- a runtime flow map
- a storage topology design
- a project-management roadmap
- a README

It is a bounded continuity emission plan.

## 2. Why This Plan Is Needed Now

Continuity now has overview, emission contract, body schema, conformance posture, and runtime flow.

That means continuity emitter and preservation work should not begin without a bounded emission plan. The body now has enough law to say what the first v1 continuity emission round is supposed to form, check, optionally carry, preserve, and interpret.

Current root continuity emission behavior remains lineage evidence, not automatic v1 law. The root line shows the historically real v0 continuity path, but that run-centric posture does not by itself define how v1 continuity should be emitted.

## 3. First Principle

Continuity emission must emit what has been lawfully formed.

Continuity body comes before explicit execution relation.  
Explicit execution relation comes before continuity preservation.  
Later carrying or preservation must not redefine continuity body.

In v1 terms, continuity must first stand as continuity body before any execution-related carrying can be judged, and both must stand before preservation can be read as lawful preservation.

## 4. Scope of the First v1 Continuity Emission Round

The first v1 continuity emission round is centered on the continuity turn as the in-scope continuity artifact for this round.

This scope is intentionally bounded. It is continuity-specific, not a reopening of the whole ordinary artifact round. The five ordinary canonical body families have already been handled through their own law, schema, conformance, and emission surfaces.

This first continuity round exists only to establish the bounded v1 posture for continuity turn emission under current continuity law.

## 5. Emission Layers

The first v1 continuity emission round distinguishes three continuity-specific output layers:

- continuity body output
- optional explicit execution-relation output
- continuity preservation output

Continuity body output is the continuity turn as continuity artifact.

Optional explicit execution-relation output is the lawful case in which continuity body may travel together with explicit execution or envelope relation while remaining separable from it.

Continuity preservation output is the preserved result of placing already formed continuity output into preservation context.

These layers may appear in one continuity round. They must not collapse into one undifferentiated continuity output surface.

## 6. Ordered Continuity Emission Path

The lawful order of the first continuity emission round is:

1. canonical continuity body formation  
2. canonical continuity body conformance  
3. optional explicit execution-relation carrying  
4. execution-relation conformance  
5. continuity preservation  
6. continuity preservation conformance  
7. fixture interpretation where relevant

Continuity embodiment must follow this order rather than collapse it.

Continuity body formation and continuity body conformance come first because continuity must stand as continuity body before any later carried execution relation can be judged.

Execution-relation carrying, where present, comes later because explicit relation is downstream of continuity body standing rather than a substitute for it.

Continuity preservation and continuity preservation conformance come later again because preservation must not redefine what continuity already was.

Fixture interpretation comes after the relevant continuity conformance surface because fixture meaning depends on what continuity stage and layer were actually exercised.

## 7. First Continuity Proof Slice

The first bounded continuity proof slice should be small and decisive.

At minimum, it should contain:

- at least one positive lawful continuity path
- at least one bounded negative or refusal-path continuity example where useful
- enough emitted structure to prove that v1 continuity can operate without collapsing continuity body, execution relation, and preservation

The positive continuity path should demonstrate that continuity can stand as append-oriented, artifact-linked continuity subordinate to preserved canonical reality.

The bounded negative or refusal-path example, where used, should demonstrate that continuity-specific failure can remain explicit at a specific continuity layer without being misread as total architectural incoherence.

This first slice does not settle all future continuity work. It is the first bounded proof that v1 continuity can operate under current law without falling back into v0 collapse patterns.

## 8. Anchor and Predecessor Posture

Continuity emission must preserve canonical artifact anchoring.

Predecessor relation may support append-oriented continuity by making lawful succession explicit across continuity turns.

Predecessor relation must not silently replace canonical artifact anchoring. Continuity remains subordinate to preserved canonical reality rather than to generic event history, generic runtime logging, or pure sequence display.

The first continuity emission round must therefore preserve the difference between:

- canonical artifact anchoring
- predecessor succession

Both may matter. They must not be flattened into one atmospheric continuity signal.

## 9. Current-v0 / Desired-v1 Distinction

Current root continuity emission behavior is historically real v0 lineage.

That root posture shows how continuity was actually emitted under a run-centric path above preserved canonical reality. It remains important as source-side evidence.

The desired v1 continuity emission plan is the target posture that later continuity emitter work must serve. This is not blame language. It is the explicit statement that v1 continuity emission is being re-derived rather than silently inherited from the root line’s convenience path.

## 10. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming first v1 continuity emission round must not allow:

- execution-first continuity emission posture
- predecessor-only continuity posture that severs anchor relation
- combined output that destroys recoverability of continuity body versus execution relation
- preservation-first definition of continuity
- reuse of v0 run-centric posture by convenience

It must also not allow positive continuity proof to be claimed from carried execution relation alone, from preservation alone, or from generic sequence appearance alone.

## 11. Downstream Consequences

Later continuity emitter re-derivation must follow this emission plan.

Later continuity fixture generation must follow this emission plan. Later continuity preservation handling must follow this emission plan.

This file is the continuity embodiment bridge from continuity runtime law into controlled emitter work. It requires later continuity implementation surfaces to preserve the stage order and layer distinctions already established in current v1 continuity law.

## 12. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines the bounded continuity emission plan only.

Later files or implementation work may realize this plan in continuity emitter and preservation surfaces. This plan exists so v1 continuity can move into emitter re-derivation without re-importing v0 collapse patterns.
