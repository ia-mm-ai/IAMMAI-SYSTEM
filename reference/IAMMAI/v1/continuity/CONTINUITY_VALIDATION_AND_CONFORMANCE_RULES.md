# IAMMAI v1 Continuity Validation and Conformance Rules

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the bounded v1 continuity-specific validation and conformance posture.

Its job is to state how conformance is evaluated across canonical continuity body, any explicit continuity-related execution or envelope relation, and continuity preservation context without collapsing those layers into one undifferentiated continuity validation surface.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a continuity schema
- a continuity emitter patch
- a continuity validator implementation note
- a runtime flow map for the whole repo
- a storage topology design
- a project-management roadmap
- a README

It is a bounded continuity contract.

## 2. Why This File Is Needed Now

Continuity has now been reopened explicitly in v1.

Continuity now has an overview, an emission contract, and a canonical body schema. That means continuity-specific conformance must now be stated before continuity-specific runtime flow, emitter, and preservation work proceed.

Continuity remains a special case. It therefore cannot simply inherit the ordinary artifact conformance posture without additional continuity-specific clarification.

## 3. First Principle

Continuity conformance must evaluate what continuity is actually claiming.

Continuity body, execution relation, and preservation context are distinct conformance surfaces.  
No later layer may silently redefine continuity body by convenience.

In v1 terms, continuity conformance must keep continuity subordinate to preserved canonical reality rather than letting continuity become generic history, generic runtime logging, or grouped preservation residue.

## 4. Continuity Conformance Surfaces

This contract distinguishes three bounded continuity conformance surfaces:

- canonical continuity body conformance
- continuity execution or envelope relation conformance
- continuity preservation conformance

Canonical continuity body conformance is responsible for whether the continuity turn stands as continuity body under current v1 continuity law and the v1 canonical continuity body schema.

Continuity execution or envelope relation conformance is responsible for whether any explicit execution relation carried with continuity remains explicit, bounded, and non-collapsed with the continuity body.

Continuity preservation conformance is responsible for whether preserved continuity remains readable, reconstructable, and non-redefining in preservation context.

These surfaces may relate in one later continuity path. They must still remain distinct.

## 5. Canonical Continuity Body Conformance

The body schema validates canonical continuity body only.

Canonical continuity body conformance must therefore confirm append-oriented, artifact-linked, subordinate continuity. It asks whether the continuity turn stands as a bounded continuity artifact linked to preserved canonical reality rather than as generic history.

Body conformance does not validate execution relation or preservation context. Passing body schema does not by itself prove lawful execution relation or lawful preservation.

## 6. Anchor and Predecessor Posture

Continuity body must remain anchored to preserved canonical artifacts rather than to generic history.

Canonical artifact-anchor relation is therefore part of continuity standing. Continuity is lawful where it remains linked to preserved canonical artifacts that actually anchor the continuity turn.

Predecessor relation may support append-oriented continuity. It may help make lawful succession explicit across continuity turns.

Predecessor relation alone must not silently replace canonical artifact anchoring if the body schema does not allow that. Continuity conformance must read anchor relation and predecessor relation explicitly rather than atmospherically.

## 7. Execution-Relation Conformance

Where continuity later travels with explicit execution or envelope relation, that relation must be validated separately from continuity body.

Execution relation may matter more directly here than in the ordinary artifact families because continuity remains more entangled with execution occurrence.

That relation must still remain explicit. It must not silently become continuity identity by default, and it must not be treated as though continuity body had already absorbed execution occurrence merely because the v0 line historically did so.

## 8. Preservation Conformance

Preservation of continuity must remain distinct from continuity body.

Preservation may preserve continuity body and explicit execution relation together where both are lawfully present. Preservation conformance must then ask whether that preserved result remains readable by layer and recoverable without guesswork.

Preservation conformance must not redefine continuity by grouping, placement, or retrieval convenience. Preservation success does not rewrite what continuity body was, and it does not repair earlier body or execution collapse by storage appearance alone.

## 9. Fixture and Failure Posture

Continuity-positive fixtures should demonstrate lawful continuity standing.

Bounded negative fixtures may demonstrate continuity-specific failure without implying total architectural incoherence. If continuity-specific refusal or bounded failure is exercised later, failure must be read by layer:

- body failure
- execution-relation failure
- preservation failure

No continuity failure should be flattened into one generic pass or fail impression. A bounded failure at one continuity surface is not the same thing as total collapse of the continuity line.

## 10. Current-v0 / Desired-v1 Distinction

Root continuity behavior remains historically real v0 lineage.

V0 continuity conformance was shaped by run-centric posture. That source-side history remains important because it shows how continuity was actually emitted and read in the root line.

V1 continuity conformance must be re-derived explicitly rather than silently inherited from that convenience path. This is not blame language. It is the explicit statement that continuity cannot keep v0 run-centric assumptions merely by renaming them more cleanly.

## 11. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming v1 continuity posture must not allow:

- treating continuity as generic event history
- treating execution relation as default continuity identity
- treating preservation grouping as continuity law
- predecessor-only reading that severs continuity from preserved canonical reality
- collapsing body, execution relation, and preservation into one undifferentiated continuity pass or fail

It must also not allow continuity conformance to be satisfied by atmospheric sequence alone, by grouped storage alone, or by inherited v0 convenience alone.

## 12. Downstream Consequences

Later continuity runtime flow work must follow these rules.

Later continuity emitter work must follow these rules. Later continuity preservation handling must follow these rules.

This file is the bounded continuity conformance bridge before continuity-specific runtime and emitter work. It requires later continuity work to preserve explicit distinction between continuity body, execution relation, and preservation context rather than letting those layers collapse again during implementation.

## 13. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines the v1 continuity validation and conformance posture only.

Later files may realize these rules in continuity runtime, emitter, and preservation surfaces. This file exists so continuity can continue through v1 without re-importing v0 collapse patterns.
