# IAMMAI v1 Continuity Emission Contract

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the bounded v1 continuity emission contract.

Its job is to bridge the v1 continuity overview into later continuity-specific schema, validation, emission, and preservation work by stating how continuity body, continuity-related execution or envelope context, and continuity preservation context relate under the current v1 line.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a continuity schema
- a continuity validator implementation note
- a continuity emitter patch
- a runtime flow map for the whole repo
- a storage topology design
- a project-management roadmap
- a README

It is a bounded continuity contract.

## 2. Why This Contract Is Needed Now

Continuity has now been reopened as a special bounded v1 surface.

It remains the unresolved special case after the ordinary proof slice. The ordinary artifact families have already received body law, body schemas, conformance posture, runtime flow, emission planning, operator posture, and preservation posture. Continuity has not yet been re-derived at that same level.

Later continuity schema, emission, validation, and preservation work therefore needs an explicit contract before any of those surfaces are re-derived.

## 3. First Principle

Continuity must preserve lawful continuity, not generic history.

Continuity body, execution relation, and preservation context must remain distinct.  
Execution convenience must not silently define canonical continuity identity.

In v1 terms, continuity may preserve relation to execution occurrence where that relation is actually needed, but it must do so without allowing execution occurrence to become continuity identity by default.

## 4. Contract Layers

This contract distinguishes three continuity-specific layers:

- continuity body
- continuity-related execution or envelope context
- continuity preservation context

Continuity body is the bounded continuity artifact as artifact.

Continuity-related execution or envelope context is the explicit relation between continuity and the execution occurrence, carried context, or bounded runtime relation that may need to remain visible for lawful continuity handling.

Continuity preservation context is the bounded preservation placement through which continuity body, and any explicit execution relation carried with it, becomes durable and recoverable.

These layers may relate. They must not collapse.

## 5. Continuity Body

For continuity to stand as continuity body is for continuity to stand as a bounded continuity artifact in its own right.

Continuity body is append-oriented, artifact-linked, and subordinate to preserved canonical reality. It exists to preserve lawful accumulation or lawful continuity relation where that relation belongs to the body.

Continuity body is not generic event history, generic runtime logging, or a summary fiction above preserved artifacts.

At high level, continuity identity identifies the bounded continuity artifact as artifact. It is not identical to execution occurrence identity, and it is not identical to preservation identity.

Continuity body may remain artifact-linked and append-oriented by referencing the lawful preserved relations it actually needs. It must not do so in a way that silently turns execution occurrence into continuity identity by default.

## 6. Execution Relation

For continuity to preserve relation to execution occurrence is for continuity to preserve the bounded execution relation that may matter more directly here than in the five ordinary canonical body families.

That relation may remain important because continuity is concerned with lawful becoming across runs or turns, and the root continuity line was historically run-centric.

Execution occurrence identity therefore remains distinct from continuity identity. It identifies execution occurrence, not the continuity artifact as artifact.

Where continuity preserves execution relation, that relation must remain explicit. It may inform continuity. It must not silently become canonical continuity identity by default, and it must not be smuggled back in under cleaner wording.

## 7. Emission Posture

To emit continuity body as continuity body is to emit continuity in its own bounded identity as continuity artifact.

Continuity may also be emitted together with explicit execution or envelope relation where lawful. That is the bounded case in which continuity body and continuity-related execution context travel together.

Combined continuity emission must not flatten body and execution relation into one undifferentiated shape. If continuity body and execution relation travel together, their distinction must remain explicit and separable.

## 8. Preservation Posture

To preserve continuity lawfully is to preserve what already stood as continuity body, and where relevant what stood as explicit continuity-related execution relation.

Preservation may therefore preserve continuity body and explicit execution relation together where that relation is lawfully part of the preserved result.

Preservation must not redefine continuity by placement, grouping, or retrieval convenience. It must not turn grouped storage into continuity identity, and it must not use preservation context to hide whether continuity body and execution relation were ever distinct.

## 9. Current-v0 / Desired-v1 Distinction

The root continuity posture remains historically real v0 lineage.

The v0 continuity line was run-centric. It remains important as source-side evidence of how the body actually emitted continuity above preserved canonical reality.

That root posture does not settle the v1 continuity line. V1 continuity must be re-derived explicitly rather than silently inherited from that convenience path.

This is not blame language. It is the explicit statement that v1 continuity cannot lawfully solve its special-case status by keeping run-saturated continuity identity under cleaner wording.

## 10. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming v1 continuity posture must not allow:

- treating continuity as generic event history
- silent re-import of run-saturated identity through cleaner wording
- collapsing continuity body into execution context
- collapsing continuity into preservation context
- using continuity to stand in for missing canonical artifacts
- continuity schema work on top of unresolved body or execution collapse

## 11. Downstream Consequences

Later continuity schema work must follow this contract.

Later continuity validation and conformance work must follow this contract. Later continuity emission handling must follow this contract. Later continuity preservation handling must follow this contract.

This file is the bounded bridge before continuity-specific schema and emitter work. It requires later continuity work to keep continuity body, execution relation, and preservation context explicit rather than letting the v0 run-centric posture quietly re-enter as default structure.

## 12. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines the v1 continuity emission contract only.

Later files may re-derive continuity schema, validation, emission, and preservation surfaces. This contract exists so continuity can re-enter v1 operatively without re-importing v0 collapse patterns.
