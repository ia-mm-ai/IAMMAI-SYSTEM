# IAMMAI v1 Continuity Operator Runbook

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the bounded operator-level runbook for the first controlled v1 continuity proof slice.

Its job is to state how the first v1 continuity proof slice is to be exercised and read at operator level, without becoming code and without silently importing v0 run-centric posture.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a continuity schema
- a continuity emitter implementation patch
- a continuity validator code note
- a runtime flow map
- a storage topology design
- a project-management roadmap
- a README

It is a bounded continuity operator runbook.

## 2. Why This Runbook Is Needed Now

Continuity now has overview, emission contract, body schema, conformance posture, runtime flow, and emission plan.

The first continuity proof slice therefore needs an operator-level reading before continuity emitter and preservation code are re-derived. The operator must know what continuity is exercising, in what order it is to be read, and what kinds of failure are lawful without flattening every result into one pass or fail impression.

Current root continuity behavior remains lineage evidence, not automatic v1 procedure. It shows the historically real v0 continuity path, including run-centric posture, but it does not by itself define the operator posture of the v1 continuity slice.

## 3. First Principle

The operator must exercise what has been lawfully formed.

Continuity body comes before explicit execution relation.  
Explicit execution relation comes before preservation.  
Later carrying or preservation must not redefine continuity body.

The operator therefore reads the continuity proof slice in the same order that continuity is lawfully claimed. The operator does not begin from preservation, from carried execution relation, or from historical familiarity with the root continuity path.

## 4. Scope of the First Controlled Continuity Proof Slice

The first controlled continuity proof slice is bounded to the continuity turn as the in-scope continuity artifact.

This first slice is intentionally small and decisive. It is continuity-specific rather than a reopening of the whole ordinary artifact round.

Its purpose is to show that continuity can now be exercised under current v1 continuity law without collapsing continuity body, execution relation, and preservation into one generic continuity surface.

## 5. Operator Sequence

The bounded operator-level sequence is:

1. canonical continuity body formation  
2. canonical continuity body conformance  
3. optional explicit execution-relation carrying  
4. execution-relation conformance  
5. continuity preservation  
6. continuity preservation conformance  
7. fixture interpretation

This is the lawful order of reading.

The operator first asks whether continuity was formed as continuity body. The operator then asks whether that body conforms as continuity body. Only after that may the operator read any optional carried execution relation. Only after that may the operator read preservation. Fixture meaning is read after the relevant continuity stage has already been identified.

## 6. Positive Continuity Path

The positive lawful continuity path is meant to demonstrate that the first continuity proof slice can:

- form continuity turn body as continuity body
- keep continuity body subordinate to preserved canonical reality
- keep canonical artifact anchoring explicit
- carry explicit execution relation only where lawful and without collapsing it into continuity identity
- preserve continuity without allowing preservation to redefine it

This positive path should remain small and continuity-specific. It does not prove total continuity completion. It proves continuity stage order, anchor discipline, and layer distinction in one bounded slice.

## 7. Negative and Refusal-Path Continuity Posture

The bounded negative or refusal-path continuity example is meant to demonstrate that explicit bounded continuity failure can remain lawful.

In this first continuity proof slice, bounded failure may include continuity body non-conformance, execution-relation non-conformance, preservation non-conformance, or a lawful refusal-path result where a continuity boundary is expected to hold by non-admission rather than by passage.

Refusal-path continuity outcome is not the same as architectural incoherence. If the exercised continuity boundary was intentionally constructed to refuse, then refusal may be the lawful result rather than evidence of collapse.

## 8. Reading Outcomes by Layer

The operator must read outcomes by layer:

- continuity body outcome
- execution-relation outcome
- continuity preservation outcome
- fixture interpretation outcome

Continuity body outcome answers whether the continuity turn stands as continuity body.

Execution-relation outcome answers whether any carried execution relation remained explicit and did not collapse into continuity identity.

Continuity preservation outcome answers whether preservation remained typed and non-redefining.

Fixture interpretation outcome answers what kind of continuity example was exercised and whether its success, failure, or refusal was expected at that continuity layer.

The operator must not flatten these into one undifferentiated success or failure judgment.

## 9. Anchor and Predecessor Reading

The operator must distinguish canonical artifact anchoring from predecessor succession.

Predecessor succession may support append-oriented continuity by making lawful succession explicit across continuity turns.

Predecessor succession must not silently replace anchor relation. Continuity must remain subordinate to preserved canonical reality rather than to generic event history, generic runtime logging, or atmospheric sequence.

The operator therefore reads:

- anchor relation as the continuity relation to preserved canonical artifacts
- predecessor succession as the continuity relation to prior lawful continuity turns

These relations may coexist. They must not be flattened into one generic continuity signal.

## 10. Stop Conditions

The operator must stop when continuity body does not stand.

The operator must stop when carried execution relation breaks body or execution distinction.

The operator must stop when preservation redefines continuity rather than preserving what already stood.

The operator may record a lawful refusal-path result without treating it as continuity collapse when:

- the refusal was bounded and explicit
- the exercised continuity stage or layer was the one expected to refuse
- the refusal did not silently blur continuity body, execution relation, or preservation distinction

Stopping is therefore continuity-stage-aware. Not every non-passage is collapse. But collapse at the exercised continuity stage must stop the slice rather than being softened into atmosphere.

## 11. Current-v0 / Desired-v1 Distinction

Current root continuity runs remain historically real v0 lineage.

They preserve the fact that the root line actually emitted and read continuity through a run-centric posture closely tied to execution occurrence.

This runbook defines the desired v1 continuity operator posture that later emitter work must serve. This is not blame language toward the root line. It is the explicit statement that v1 continuity proof execution is being read under newly articulated continuity law rather than under inherited v0 convenience.

## 12. Anti-Collapse Rules

A conforming continuity operator posture must not allow:

- execution-first continuity operator posture
- predecessor-only reading that severs continuity from canonical artifact anchoring
- preservation-first reading of continuity success
- combined output treated as if continuity body standing were already settled
- flattening of stage-specific failure into total incoherence

It must also not allow historically real root continuity behavior to override v1 continuity law merely because that earlier path already exists in the repository.

## 13. Downstream Consequences

Later continuity emitter re-derivation must support this runbook.

Later continuity fixture generation must support this runbook. Later continuity preservation handling must support this runbook.

This file is the continuity embodiment bridge from plan into controlled proof execution. It states how the first continuity proof slice is to be exercised and read before concrete continuity emitter and preservation re-derivation begins.

Later files or implementation work may realize this runbook in continuity emitter and preservation surfaces. This runbook exists so v1 continuity can enter controlled proof execution without re-importing v0 collapse patterns.
