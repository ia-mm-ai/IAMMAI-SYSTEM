# IAMMAI v0 to v1 Transfer Account

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first bounded transfer account for the lawful repo-scale transition from the frozen root `v0` source-side body into the `v1/` derivation line.

Its job is to state, at class level, what crosses from `v0` into `v1` as source-bound authority, source-side evidence, or re-derived surface.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a fixture taxonomy
- a standing-criteria note
- a runtime contract
- a schema file
- a README
- a project-management roadmap

It is a bounded transfer account.

## 2. First Principle

Transfer from `v0` to `v1` must remain explicit.

Nothing crosses by implication.  
What remains source-bound must stay source-bound.  
What is re-derived must be named as re-derived, not disguised as unchanged inheritance.

## 3. Why a Transfer Account Is Needed

Without an explicit account, `v1` can inherit by atmosphere.

Flat copying would falsify lineage. Silent rewording would falsify history. A later line could begin to appear as though it simply owns the earlier body, even where the lawful relation is actually retention of source authority, preservation of evidence, or explicit downstream re-derivation.

The transfer account exists to keep repo-scale transition non-fraudulent.

## 4. Transfer Classes

At class level, the transfer account distinguishes:

- source-side authority retained from `v0`
- source-side evidence and lineage retained from `v0`
- surfaces selected for explicit re-derivation in `v1`

Source-side authority retained from `v0` includes the constitutional source and those stable doctrinal or architectural distinctions that remain governing unless later explicitly superseded.

Source-side evidence and lineage retained from `v0` includes proof artifacts, experiments, traces, and historically real explanatory or enforcement surfaces that remain important for lineage without automatically standing as ongoing `v1` authority.

Surfaces selected for explicit re-derivation in `v1` include those classes where later law, schema, enforcement, emitted shape, or explanatory posture must be restated under the new line rather than merely copied.

## 5. What Counts As Lawful Transfer

The following count as lawful transfer:

- explicit retention
- explicit reference
- explicit re-derivation
- explicit supersession when later needed

These are the lawful transfer forms because they keep the relation between source, evidence, and downstream restatement visible.

## 6. What Does Not Count As Lawful Transfer

The following do not count as lawful transfer:

- silent copy as if unchanged authority
- hidden mutation of `v0`
- vague inheritance by adjacency
- editorial cleanup presented as original form
- reuse of old proof as automatic proof of `v1`

## 7. Evidentiary Posture

`v0` remains the source-side lineage body.

`v1` must later produce its own proof where standing is claimed. The transfer account preserves relation between source, evidence, and re-derived line so the new line can remain connected to `v0` without pretending that inherited visibility is the same thing as downstream proof.

## 8. Anti-Collapse Rules

The following must not occur:

- transfer by atmosphere
- source erasure
- flat copying that destroys authority ranking
- re-derived surface pretending to be original source
- standing by transfer account alone

## 9. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines the transfer-account posture only.

Later `v1` files and fresh proof may populate this account more concretely. The transfer account exists so `v1` can emerge lawfully without falsifying `v0`.
