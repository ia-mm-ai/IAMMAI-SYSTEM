# IAMMAI v1 Fixture Taxonomy

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the first explicit v1 taxonomy for fixture classes.

Its job is to distinguish positive examples, refusal-path artifacts, illegibility cases, conformance-failure examples, and other intentionally broken materials so they are not read flatly or mistaken for canonical output.

It does not define:

- a constitutional rewrite
- a seam declaration
- an authority or inheritance map
- an artifact-boundary decision
- a schema
- a runtime contract
- a README
- a full test plan

It is a bounded taxonomy note.

## 2. First Principle

Fixture class must be explicit.

Positive and negative examples must not collapse into one evidentiary kind.  
Fixture status does not equal canonical standing.  
Broken-path material may be lawful as a fixture while non-conformant as an artifact example.

## 3. Why Taxonomy Is Needed

Without explicit taxonomy, successful examples, refusals, illegibility cases, and broken artifacts can be misread flatly.

Flat reading produces false contradiction and false drift. A lawful refusal can be mistaken for accidental corruption. A broken local test artifact can be mistaken for canonical emitted output. A successful example can be mistaken for constitutional source or permanent downstream authority.

Taxonomy exists to preserve evidentiary clarity.

## 4. Positive Fixtures

Positive fixtures are examples intended to show lawful passage, lawful receipt, or other successful bounded behavior.

They are:

- useful evidence
- not constitutional source
- not automatically permanent v1 authority

Positive fixtures help later schema, runtime, and emitter work because they show what a bounded successful path looked like. They remain subordinate to constitutional source, stable doctrine, and later ratified v1 enforcement surfaces.

## 5. Negative Fixtures

Negative fixtures are examples intentionally used to show lawful non-passage, refusal, illegibility, or bounded failure.

They are:

- expected to fail in a specific way
- not accidental corruption by default
- important evidence of anti-collapse behavior

Negative fixtures matter because a lawful body must preserve visible refusal and bounded failure without smoothing them into successful output.

## 6. Refusal-Path Fixtures

Refusal-path fixtures are the subclass that shows lawful rejection at a boundary.

In this class:

- crossing is rejected lawfully
- no destination receipt is created
- no fake continuity is claimed

These fixtures are important because they preserve what non-admission looked like without allowing refusal-path material to impersonate accepted downstream output.

## 7. Illegibility Fixtures

Illegibility fixtures are the subclass that intentionally breaks legibility conditions for bounded testing.

In this class, one or more required legibility conditions are intentionally broken, such as:

- source legibility
- type legibility
- other required legibility conditions

These fixtures exist to show that lawful passage depends on explicit legibility rather than on assumption, cleanup, or interpretive rescue.

## 8. Conformance-Failure Fixtures

Conformance-failure fixtures are examples intentionally used to demonstrate mismatch against current schema or conformance expectations, or against other explicit closure rules once those closure surfaces are ratified.

They are bounded negative examples. They are not accidental drift by default, and they must not be read as canonical outputs merely because they are structurally close to positive examples.

## 9. Lab-Only Broken Artifacts

Lab-only broken artifacts are artifacts intentionally broken for local experimental proof.

They may be useful for seam, refusal, illegibility, or emitter-boundary testing, but they must not be mistaken for canonical emitted outputs. Their status is experimental and classificatory, not authoritative.

## 10. Handling Rules

The handling rules are:

- fixtures must be explicitly classified
- negative fixtures must not be mixed visually or semantically with ordinary canonical output
- positive fixtures still remain evidence, not constitutional source
- taxonomy exists so later v1 surfaces can inherit examples lawfully, not by confusion

This means fixture handling must stay subordinate to constitutional source, stable doctrine, and later ratified v1 enforcement surfaces rather than letting examples decide law by convenience.

## 11. Closing Boundary Statement

This file defines fixture classes only.

Later schema, runtime, and emitter files may use this taxonomy. The taxonomy exists so v1 can distinguish proof, refusal, illegibility, and drift without flattening them into one kind of artifact.
