# Cross-Carrier Divergence Boundary V0 Minimum Specification

## 1. Purpose

This file defines the bounded boundary for preserving cross-carrier divergence in the present `IAMMAI-SYSTEM` execution line.

Cross-carrier divergence is a visible condition across carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence.

It may appear as mismatch, conflict, disagreement, blocked posture, basis mismatch, outcome mismatch, integrity mismatch, role mismatch, or non-claim conflict between selected carrier evidence surfaces.

The purpose of this boundary is to prevent disagreement between carriers, carrier emissions, returned evidence, admitted evidence, receipt evidence, or refusal evidence from being hidden, overwritten, flattened, prematurely resolved, or converted into:

- source replacement
- currentness creation
- authority
- permission
- truth
- action
- carrier hierarchy
- multi-carrier law
- distributed standing
- continuation

Divergence is not collapse by default.

Hidden divergence is collapse.

This file is a boundary specification only. It is not an implementation, resolver, test, registry, workflow, router, roadmap, README, or manifesto.

## 3. Why This Spec Is Needed Now

The carrier role and emission boundary defines local carrier roles and permitted emissions.

The carrier-local emission admission boundary defines how carrier-local emissions may enter the body line as evidence only.

The cross-carrier surface receipt boundary proves that one carried surface may be received by one carrier as carried evidence without creating source, currentness, authority, permission, successor standing, body formation, multi-carrier law, distributed standing, or continuation.

The cross-surface correspondence boundary permits selected standing surfaces to be read together while preserving rank, source, scope, lineage, local outcome, and non-claims.

Returned receipt evidence and returned refusal evidence have become visible downstream evidence.

Current self-orientation v7 mirrors returned carrier evidence and visible refusal evidence as downstream posture.

Current-body conformance v2 verifies that the v7-integrated posture coheres as one current body line without opening carrier relation, currentness, authority, permission, signal, presence, threshold, truth, action, consequence, multi-carrier law, distributed standing, or continuation.

The body has not yet defined what happens when carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence diverges.

Divergence must be bounded before cross-carrier currentness can be considered.

This boundary is still narrower than multi-carrier law. It records how carrier evidence may disagree without collapse. It does not define carrier relation, distributed standing, cross-carrier currentness, carrier registry, repository synchronization, full body transfer, second-body creation, or successor carrier law.

## 4. Definitions

`carrier-produced evidence` means an operation-local output emitted by a carrier under a declared carrier role, including receipt, refusal, manifest, integrity evidence, transfer declaration, or returned evidence packet where bounded.

`carrier-admitted evidence` means carrier-produced or returned evidence that has been admitted into the body line as downstream evidence by a bounded admission surface.

`cross-carrier divergence` means a visible mismatch, conflict, disagreement, blocked posture, basis mismatch, outcome mismatch, integrity mismatch, role mismatch, emission mismatch, return-path mismatch, or non-claim conflict between selected carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence surfaces.

`divergence basis` means the selected carrier evidence identities, carrier identities where available, outcomes, source or carried basis where available, divergence question, divergence type, visible mismatch, and explicit non-claims that permit divergence to be recorded.

`divergence claim` means the bounded assertion that selected carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence surfaces diverge for one declared comparison.

`visible divergence` means the mismatch remains preserved, inspectable, and attributable to the selected evidence surfaces. Visible divergence does not decide which evidence is correct.

`hidden divergence` means mismatch is obscured, overwritten, flattened, erased, omitted by convenience selection, or treated as if it did not exist.

`divergence refusal` means a requested divergence recording is refused because the selected evidence, declared question, divergence type, basis, or non-claims are missing, malformed, unsupported, contradictory, or collapse-shaped.

`non-divergence` means selected carrier evidence does not diverge for the declared divergence question while each selected surface remains preserved in its own local posture.

`divergence preservation` means the selected mismatch remains visible without resolving it into source, currentness, authority, permission, truth, action, carrier hierarchy, multi-carrier law, distributed standing, or continuation.

`divergence consequence boundary` means the limited downstream effect of recorded divergence. Divergence may block reliance or require later bounded review, but it does not decide source, currentness, truth, action, carrier correctness, or carrier relation.

## 5. What Divergence Means

Divergence means one or more selected carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence surfaces do not align for a declared comparison.

Divergence may include:

- outcome mismatch
- receipt/refusal mismatch
- basis mismatch
- hash or integrity mismatch
- carrier role mismatch
- carrier identity mismatch
- return path mismatch
- emission class mismatch
- non-claim conflict
- stale or sequence mismatch
- missing evidence in one carrier line
- malformed evidence in one carrier line
- blocked evidence in one carrier line
- declared posture conflict

Divergence means mismatch is preserved for later bounded assessment.

Divergence may be recorded as evidence.

Divergence does not invalidate selected carrier evidence by default.

Divergence does not decide which carrier is correct.

## 10. Divergence Preservation Rules

Divergent evidence must remain visible.

Blocked evidence may be preserved as divergence evidence.

Successful evidence does not erase blocked evidence.

Blocked evidence does not invalidate successful evidence by default.

One carrier's refusal does not invalidate another carrier's receipt by default.

One carrier's receipt does not erase another carrier's refusal.

Mismatch must not be hidden by selecting only the convenient surface.

Divergence may block downstream reliance until bounded.

Divergence does not create currentness.

Divergence does not create a winning carrier.

Divergence does not create carrier hierarchy.

Divergence may:

- prevent downstream reliance
- require later correspondence review
- require later admission review
- require later currentness boundary review
- remain open as visible divergence
- be used as evidence for a future block

Divergence may not:

- decide source
- decide currentness
- decide truth
- decide action
- select a winning carrier
- erase losing carrier evidence
- create carrier hierarchy
- create carrier relation
- create multi-carrier law
- create distributed standing
- authorize continuation

## 12. Relation to Carrier Role and Emission Boundary

The carrier role and emission boundary defines what a carrier may locally be and emit under a declared operation.

Divergence may occur between emissions from admitted carrier roles.

Divergence may also name pressure involving candidate carrier roles, but unsupported role or unsupported emission posture must block before divergence is recorded as standing evidence.

Role mismatch or emission mismatch may be divergence only when selected evidence is otherwise admissible for the declared divergence review.

Divergence does not grant a carrier any new role.

Divergence does not grant new emission rights.

Divergence does not make a role permanent identity.

## 13. Relation to Carrier-Local Emission Admission

Carrier-local emission admission brings carrier-local emission into the body line as evidence only.

Divergence review may compare admitted carrier-local evidence.

Returned-but-not-admitted evidence may be considered only when the divergence question explicitly permits that bounded review and preserves that returned evidence is not admitted by return alone.

Admission does not create divergence by itself.

Divergence does not admit evidence by itself.

Divergence does not convert admitted evidence into source, currentness, authority, permission, successor, body, signal, presence, threshold, truth, action, consequence, multi-carrier law, distributed standing, or continuation.

Basis mismatch may be recorded as `BASIS_DIVERGENCE`.

Receipt divergence does not invalidate the source surface by default.

Receipt divergence does not invalidate another carrier's receipt by default.

Receipt divergence does not create currentness.

Successful receipt does not erase blocked receipt.

Blocked receipt does not erase successful receipt.

## 15. Relation to Cross-Surface Correspondence

Cross-surface correspondence may record alignment, mismatch, dependency, lineage reference, closure alignment, refusal visibility, or non-correspondence under a bounded reading relation.

The cross-carrier divergence boundary records mismatch or conflict between carrier-produced or carrier-admitted evidence.

Correspondence and divergence are related but not identical.

Correspondence does not resolve divergence.

Divergence does not replace correspondence.

Divergence does not create correspondence automatically.

Future surfaces may use both correspondence and divergence where separately justified and bounded.

Conformance over divergence does not create multi-carrier law.
