# Matter-Bound Candidate Enactment Request Admission V0 Minimum Specification

## 1. Purpose

This file defines one minimum non-executable boundary for deciding whether one exact candidate configuration may become the exact represented object of one bounded enactment request for one exact matter.

The boundary answers only:

> May one exact candidate configuration, supported by one exact family-owned candidate-admissibility or candidate-eligibility basis, be bound to one exact represented proposition for one exact matter and admitted as the exact object of one bounded enactment request for possible later, separately bounded enactment-authorization review?

The admitted object is the request.

Request admission is not candidate admission, candidate standing, enactment authorization, invocation permission, execution, enactment occurrence, result, trace, evidence allocation, evidence adequacy, proposition evaluation, truth, currentness, authority, adoption, integration, reuse, continuation, or successor force.

This specification does not admit or establish the candidate. It does not establish that the represented proposition is true. It does not authorize, invoke, enact, execute, schedule, or establish occurrence. It does not evaluate likely success. It does not create or allocate evidence. It does not create an automatic handoff.

This file is a specification only. It creates no resolver, test, artifact, receipt, fixture, terminal summary, index, registry, runtime, deployment, or executable surface.

## 3. Separate Jurisdiction

Existing candidate admission or evaluation may preserve one non-standing candidate under its owning family law. Existing invocation-request law may admit one request concerning already-selected executable machinery. Existing authorization law may separately record one-shot authorization without invoking or executing. Existing occurrence-evidence law may separately allocate evidence to one exact occurrence claim without migration into a represented proposition.

Those distinct functions do not self-compose into candidate-specific enactment-request admission.

This boundary decides only whether one exact supplied request envelope is sufficiently singular, bounded, attributable, contract-supported, internally coherent, and non-converting to become a candidate for possible later, separately bounded enactment-authorization review.

This boundary does not:

- re-admit, reevaluate, or establish the candidate;
- establish candidate standing or currentness;
- evaluate or establish the represented proposition;
- authorize enactment or invocation;
- execute or enact the candidate;
- establish an occurrence, output, result, trace, or success posture;
- determine evidence adequacy or allocate evidence;

- create a schedule, automatic continuation, or successor authority.

## 5. Bounded Topology

One review must keep exactly these elements distinct:

1. one exact matter `M`;
2. one exact candidate configuration `C`;
3. one exact family-owned candidate-admissibility or candidate-eligibility contract `EC`;
4. one exact supplied candidate result or basis `CA` under `EC` for the declared use;
5. one exact represented proposition `P`;
6. one exact directional candidate-to-proposition binding `B(C,P)`;
7. one exact enactment request `Q`;
8. one exact requested enactment identity `X`;
9. one exact requested enactment scope `S`;
10. one exact proposed input set `I`;
11. one exact proposed condition set `K`;
12. one exact requested locality or destination `L`, where applicable; and
13. one invocation count equal to exactly one.

The request-admission relation is:

`M + C + EC + CA + P + B(C,P) + Q + X + S + I + K + L + invocation_count(1) -> request_admission_decision`

This notation expresses exact selection and binding only. It does not express enactment authorization, invocation permission, execution, occurrence, causal force, proposition support, truth, currentness, standing, authority, adoption, integration, reuse, continuation, or successor force.

## 6. Definitions

`exact matter` means one declared matter identity, purpose, bounded scope, exact request-admission question, and outside boundary. It is not a candidate population, generic experiment family, repository-wide concern, or global operation class.

`candidate configuration` means one explicitly identified configuration `C` with exact id, type, source family, custody, lineage, version or immutable reference, and content identity where required by its owning contract. Within this envelope, `C` is explicitly non-standing and non-current.

`family-owned candidate-admissibility or candidate-eligibility contract` means exact `EC`, owned by the source family, that defines what qualifies as a candidate, what its result or basis means, the scope and effect of that result, and whether it is applicable to the declared enactment-request use. This boundary does not replace `EC`.

`candidate result or basis` means exact supplied `CA`, attributable to `EC` and the source family, that carries the contract-owned posture proposed as support for admitting `Q`. `CA` is basis for request admission only. It is not candidate standing, proposition evidence, enactment authorization, or execution permission.

`represented proposition` means one exact bounded statement `P` with exact id, type, wording or immutable reference, semantic owner, matter scope, and outside boundary. `P` is not evaluated by this boundary.

`candidate-to-proposition binding` means one exact directional binding `B(C,P)` stating only that exact `C` is the selected representation of exact `P` for exact `Q` in exact `M`. The binding does not establish correspondence, semantic adequacy, proposition truth, candidate standing, adoption, or integration.

`enactment request` means exact `Q`, a request to admit exact `C`, bound to exact `P`, as the object of one proposed enactment `X` under exact `S`, `I`, `K`, `L`, and invocation count one for possible later, separately bounded enactment-authorization review. `Q` is not authorization or invocation permission.

`requested enactment` means exact proposed enactment identity `X` named by `Q`. It is a request target only. No enactment event is created or established here.

`requested enactment scope` means exact `S`, the maximum proposed bounds of `X`, including the exact candidate, matter, proposition binding, inputs, conditions, invocation count, locality or destination, and outside boundary. Admission of `Q` does not approve `S` for execution.

`proposed inputs` means exact `I`, the closed input identities or immutable references proposed for `X`. Proposed inputs are not admitted execution inputs and are not occurrence evidence.

`proposed conditions` means exact `K`, the closed conditions under which authorization would later be considered. Proposed conditions are not authorization and do not establish that the conditions hold.

`requested locality or destination` means exact `L`, where applicable, identifying the proposed bounded locality or destination without creating deployment, runtime hosting, transfer, or execution authority.

`single invocation posture` means `Q` requests exactly one invocation. It creates no repeat, retry, standing invocation lane, reusable permission, or general enactment route.

`request admission` means one boundary-local decision about whether exact `Q` may become a candidate for possible later, separately bounded enactment-authorization review. The admitted object is `Q`, not `C`, `P`, `X`, or `S`.

`later enactment-authorization review` means a future, separately specified and separately admitted review that may decide whether one exact enactment authorization can be created. It is not created, selected, scheduled, or authorized here.

`standing invocation lane` means any durable or reusable posture allowing invocation without a new exact bounded authorization. This boundary creates none.

`successor force` means any automatic, inherited, continuing, reusable, or follow-on authority inferred from request admission. This boundary creates none.

- at most one exact locality or destination `L` is supplied, and it is present when the requested scope requires it;
- requested invocation count is exactly one;
- every identity and required immutable reference is complete and unique within the envelope;
- `C`, `P`, `B(C,P)`, `Q`, `X`, `S`, `I`, `K`, `L`, and `M` bind to the same exact matter and requested enactment;

- `C` remains explicitly non-standing and non-current;

- `EC` is exact, attributable to the source family, and governs exact `C`, exact `CA`, and the declared use class;

These are request-envelope checks only. Passing them does not re-admit `C`, validate `P`, approve `S`, authorize `X`, or create any later review.

## 10. Outcome Family

The boundary-local outcome family is exactly:

- `CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_ADMITTED`
- `CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_NOT_ADMITTED`
- `CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_BASIS`
- `CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_REVIEW_BLOCKED`

`CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_ADMITTED` means only that the exact supplied request envelope is sufficiently bounded and supported to become a candidate for possible later, separately bounded enactment-authorization review. It does not mean the candidate was re-admitted, the proposition was evaluated, the requested scope was approved, enactment was authorized, invocation was permitted, execution occurred, an occurrence was established, success was predicted, evidence was allocated, standing was created, or adoption or integration was allowed.

`CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_NOT_ADMITTED` means the readable and attributable supplied envelope cannot be admitted while preserving exact contract-owned applicability, matter and request coherence, one-invocation scope, and required non-claims. It does not invalidate `M`, `C`, `EC`, `CA`, `P`, `B(C,P)`, `Q`, `X`, or `S`.

`CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_BASIS` means the proposed request remains singular, bounded, attributable, and non-collapse-shaped, but one or more exact required identity, reference, contract, effect-location, applicability, basis, lineage, custody, binding, scope, input, condition, locality, or non-claim facts are absent or incomplete. It creates no request, schedule, debt, obligation, retry, or permission to obtain or resubmit that basis.

`CANDIDATE_ENACTMENT_REQUEST_REVIEW_BLOCKED` means lawful review cannot proceed because the envelope is missing, malformed, unreadable, ambiguous, contradictory, falsely attributed, unsupported, scope-widening, authority-transferring, semantic-override-shaped, selection-shaped, execution-shaped, or otherwise overreach-shaped.

All four outcomes are lawful terminal outcomes of this boundary. Negative, refused, unsupported, unresolved, or blocked content is not operational failure.

## 14. Relation to Authorization, Enactment, and Occurrence Evidence

Request admission remains upstream of any candidate-specific enactment authorization. A future authorization boundary, if separately selected and specified, would need to decide its own exact authority, scope, one-shot token, consumption, refusal, and exhaustion law. This specification supplies none of those decisions.

An admitted `Q` is not authorization `A`. It cannot be invoked, executed, consumed as authorization, or treated as permission. Any future authorization must remain separately admitted and exact.

Enactment, invocation, execution, output, result, trace, success, and occurrence remain outside this boundary. No request-admission outcome establishes that any of them exists.

Occurrence-evidence allocation remains a later separate question. If an enactment and occurrence claim are ever separately established under their own law, evidence may be allocated only under the applicable occurrence-evidence contract and only to the exact occurrence claim. Request admission creates no occurrence evidence and no evidence for `P`.

The governing downstream distinction remains:

> Evidence may establish that the candidate was enacted; it may not, by virtue of that enactment, establish what the candidate represented.
