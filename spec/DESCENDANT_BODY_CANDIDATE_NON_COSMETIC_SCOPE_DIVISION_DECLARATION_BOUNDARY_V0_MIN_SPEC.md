## 5. Definitions

`candidate non-cosmetic scope-division declaration boundary` means this boundary only, not declaration.

`candidate scope` means a prospective declared sub-scope assigned to one non-standing candidate record only if later separately bounded and recorded.

`non-cosmetic candidate A scope` means a future declared candidate A sub-scope that differs from candidate B by basis-bearing mandate, function, responsibility, or governed surface.

`non-cosmetic candidate B scope` means a future declared candidate B sub-scope that differs from candidate A by basis-bearing mandate, function, responsibility, or governed surface.

`basis-bearing scope division` means a future declared division between candidate A and candidate B scopes grounded in mandate, function, responsibility, or governed surface.

`scope-label laundering` means assigning attractive, plausible, or technical scope names that differ only as labels while treating the labels as basis-bearing division.

`cosmetic scope naming` means naming scopes differently without a non-cosmetic basis-bearing difference.

`admissible future scope declaration` means a future declaration that states candidate A scope, candidate B scope, and the basis of their division without emitting content, seal, receipt, digest, distinctness support, or standing.

`inadmissible future scope declaration` means any future declaration that relies only on id, role, label, side, order, filename, display name, variable name, assigned name, template slot, digest, hash, seal, receipt, shared evidence, operation evidence alone, or contaminated lineage as clean basis.

This boundary is permission to define a future operation shape only, not permission to declare scopes.

## 6. Boundary Thesis

Scope declaration is not scope standing.

Scope declaration is not candidate-specific basis emission.

Scope declaration is not candidate-specific content emission.

Scope declaration is not distinctness support.

Scope declaration is not candidate standing.

Naming two scopes is not basis-bearing division.

A scope label is not a scope.

A scope title is not a mandate.

A scope id is not a governed surface.

A scope digest is not evidence if the scope itself is cosmetic.

Scope division must be basis-bearing, not label-bearing.

Candidate A scope and candidate B scope must differ by mandate, function, responsibility, or governed surface.

Candidate A scope and candidate B scope must not differ only by candidate id, role, label, side, order, filename, display name, variable name, assigned name, template slot, or equivalent string replacement.
