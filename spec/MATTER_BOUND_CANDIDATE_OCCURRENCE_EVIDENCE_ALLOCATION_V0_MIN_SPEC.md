# Matter-Bound Candidate Occurrence Evidence Allocation V0 Minimum Specification

## 1. Purpose

This file defines one minimum non-executable boundary for allocating supplied occurrence evidence concerning one explicitly identified candidate configuration to one exact bounded enactment occurrence claim in one exact matter.

It answers only:

> May one explicitly identified candidate configuration be bound to one explicitly identified represented proposition for one separately authorized bounded enactment, such that resulting trace may support only the exact occurrence claim, while the enactment, output, result, success, and trace—by themselves and under this boundary—create no evidence, truth, currentness, standing, authority, adoption, or integration force for the represented proposition?

The governing distinction is:

> Evidence may establish that the candidate was enacted; it may not, by virtue of that enactment, establish what the candidate represented.

This specification does not establish that enactment occurred. It governs only where supplied evidence may be allocated if the applicable occurrence-evidence contract supports the exact occurrence claim.

This file does not enact a candidate, authorize enactment, verify occurrence evidence, establish an occurrence claim, evaluate a represented proposition, select a candidate, create a resolver, test, artifact, receipt, terminal summary, registry, catalogue, index, runtime, sandbox, local field, adoption, integration, or successor operation.

## 3. Boundary Identity and Scope

The minimum constitutional object is:

- `matter_bound_candidate_occurrence_evidence_allocation_type = MATTER_BOUND_CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION`
- `matter_bound_candidate_occurrence_evidence_allocation_version = 0.1.0`
- `matter_bound_candidate_occurrence_evidence_allocation_scope = ONE_EXACT_MATTER_ONE_EXACT_CANDIDATE_ONE_EXACT_REPRESENTED_PROPOSITION_ONE_SEPARATELY_AUTHORIZED_BOUNDED_ENACTMENT_ONE_EXACT_OCCURRENCE_CLAIM_ONLY`

The boundary is matter-bound, candidate-bound, proposition-bound, binding-bound, authorization-bound, occurrence-claim-bound, contract-bound, evidence-reference-bound, allocation-only, non-migrating, non-reusable, and read-only in constitutional effect.

One allocation decision applies only to the exact matter, candidate configuration, represented proposition, candidate-proposition binding, authorization, enactment invocation, occurrence claim, result, trace, occurrence-evidence references, and allocation statement named in one closed supplied envelope.

Another candidate, proposition, binding, authorization, invocation, occurrence claim, result, trace, matter, or downstream use requires a separate boundary-compliant envelope and separate allocation decision. No automatic successor permission is created.

## 4. Bounded Topology

One review must keep exactly these elements distinct:

1. one exact matter `M`;
2. one explicitly identified candidate configuration `C`;
3. one explicitly identified represented proposition `P`;
4. one exact candidate-to-proposition binding `B(C,P)`;
5. one separately admitted bounded enactment authorization `A`;
6. one exact bounded enactment invocation and conditions `I`;
7. one exact bounded enactment occurrence claim `O`;
8. one exact result `R`;
9. one exact trace `T`;
10. one exact applicable occurrence-evidence contract `E`;
11. supplied occurrence-evidence references `ER`; and
12. one evidence-allocation decision `D` for this enactment only.

The binding is:

`M + C + P + B(C,P) + A + I + O + R + T + E + ER -> D`

This notation expresses exact selection and binding only. It does not express truth, implication, entailment, candidate preference, occurrence establishment, proposition support, adoption, integration, authority, currentness, or standing.

The only lawful allocation target under this boundary is `O`. `P` is not an allocation target.

## 5. Definitions

`exact matter` means one declared matter identity, purpose, bounded scope, exact allocation question, and outside boundary. It is not a generic topic, experiment family, candidate population, or repository-wide concern.

`candidate configuration` means one exact enactable configuration identified by candidate id, type, version, exact reference, and content identity. Candidate configuration is not candidate standing, authority, adoption, or integration.

`represented proposition` means one exact bounded statement identified by proposition id, type, exact wording, reference, semantic owner, and matter scope. Enacting a candidate bound to the proposition does not evaluate the proposition.

`candidate-proposition binding` means one explicit relation recording that the exact candidate `C` represents, instantiates, tests, renders, or otherwise stands in the declared relation to exact proposition `P` for this enactment. The binding is descriptive input. It is not evidence that `P` is true, current, standing, authoritative, adopted, or integrated.

`bounded enactment authorization` means one separately admitted authorization `A` whose exact identity, reference, scope, inputs, conditions, invocation count, and non-reuse posture are supplied. This specification does not create or expand `A`.

`bounded enactment occurrence` means the actual event, if separately established under applicable law, of enacting exact `C` under exact `I`, inputs, conditions, and `A`. This specification does not establish that event.

`occurrence claim` means one exact wording `O` asserting only that exact `C` was enacted under cited `I`, inputs, conditions, and `A`, and that exact `R` and/or `T` was emitted as stated. It contains no proposition truth, currentness, standing, authority, adoption, or integration claim.

`occurrence evidence` means supplied evidence references whose applicable evidence contract may support exact `O`. Evidence adequacy and occurrence establishment remain governed by that contract, not by this allocation boundary.

`applicable occurrence-evidence contract` means the exact contract `E` that defines what evidence may support `O`, what adequacy means, and the scope of any contract-owned occurrence posture. This boundary may preserve an exact supplied contract-owned support posture but may not invent, reinterpret, or generalize it.

`result` means exact bounded result `R` identified by id or exact reference and result posture. Result is not success, source, authority, currentness, standing, proposition evidence, adoption, integration, or successor permission.

`trace` means exact bounded trace `T` identified by id or exact reference and preserved as audit or occurrence evidence only. Trace is not execution authority, success, source, proposition evidence, standing, currentness, adoption, integration, or reusable permission.

`evidence allocation` means one decision `D` assigning exact supplied evidence references `ER` to exact occurrence claim `O` only for this exact enactment. Allocation does not determine adequacy and does not establish occurrence.

`evidentiary migration` means treating evidence allocated to `O` as evidence for `P`, another proposition, another occurrence, another candidate, another matter, another authorization, or another use without a separate lawful basis. Evidentiary migration is prohibited.

`successor authority` means any continuing, reusable, repeatable, inherited, or follow-on authority inferred from authorization, invocation, enactment, output, result, success, trace, or allocation. This boundary creates none.

`adoption force` means any force treating enactment, result, success, repetition, trace, or allocation as a reason or permission to adopt `C` or `P`. This boundary creates none.

`integration force` means any force treating enactment, result, success, repetition, trace, or allocation as a reason or permission to integrate `C`, `P`, `R`, or `T` into standing body, runtime, local field, sandbox, registry, public surface, or successor operation. This boundary creates none.

## 6. Required Supplied Envelope

One allocation review requires one closed, explicitly selected envelope containing exactly one of each required unit and preserving at minimum:

- exact matter id, purpose, bounded scope, allocation question, and outside boundary;
- candidate `C` id, type, version, exact reference, and content identity;
- proposition `P` id, type, exact bounded statement, exact reference, semantic owner, and matter scope;
- exact `C`-to-`P` binding id, type, direction, scope, and exact bound identities;
- exact enactment or invocation id `I`;
- exact authorization `A` id, type, version where applicable, exact reference, admitted scope, permitted invocation count, and non-reuse posture;
- exact enactment inputs and conditions;
- occurrence claim `O` id, exact wording, scope, and exact references to `M`, `C`, `B(C,P)`, `A`, `I`, `R`, and `T`;
- result `R` id, exact reference, type, and bounded result posture;
- trace `T` id, exact reference, type, and audit-only or occurrence-evidence posture;
- occurrence-evidence contract `E` id, type, version where applicable, exact reference, semantic owner, exact applicability scope, and exact evidence-effect location;
- exact contract-owned allocability rule or occurrence-support posture reference showing that `ER` may support `O`;
- exact occurrence-evidence references `ER` and their binding to `O`;
- one explicit evidence-allocation statement assigning `ER` to `O` only and refusing allocation to `P`;
- exact lineage and custody references needed to preserve attribution and non-migration;
- one bounded allocation decision posture; and
- explicit non-claims.

The envelope must contain no alternatives, fallback candidate, candidate population, catalogue, candidate comparison, global proposition map, global outcome map, registry lookup, implicit latest selection, recency inference, directory-order inference, timestamp choice, sequence-number choice, or repository-wide discovery.

The envelope must not contain a second allocation target. It must not contain a proposition-evidence allocation, candidate-adoption decision, integration decision, candidate preference, or successor operation request.

## 7. Exact Binding Requirements

The envelope is coherent only when:

- every selected identity is exact and unique within the envelope;
- `B(C,P)` names the same exact `C` and `P` as the envelope;
- `A` explicitly covers the exact enactment mode, invocation, inputs, conditions, candidate, matter, and permitted count claimed by `O`;
- `I` names the same authorization, candidate, inputs, conditions, matter, result, and trace claimed by `O`;
- `O` names the same exact `C`, `A`, `I`, `R`, and `T` as the envelope;
- `O` remains an occurrence claim and contains no claim about the truth or standing of `P`;
- `R` and `T` are exact and are not invented, substituted, selected by recency, or widened beyond their references;
- `E` explicitly governs the evidence class and exact occurrence claim shape supplied for `O`;
- the contract-owned allocability rule or occurrence-support posture is exact, attributable to `E`, and permits the supplied evidence class to support exact `O`;
- every occurrence-evidence reference binds to exact `O` under `E`;
- the allocation statement names `O` as its sole evidence target and explicitly excludes `P`; and
- all non-claims remain false.

Exact binding does not establish occurrence. It preserves which claim the supplied evidence may support if `E` lawfully supports that use.

## 8. Allocation Law

The boundary preserves all of the following:

1. Adequate evidence may support only the exact occurrence claim that `C` was enacted under the cited invocation, inputs, conditions, and authorization, and that identified `R` or `T` was emitted.

2. This boundary does not determine whether supplied evidence is adequate to establish `O`. Occurrence-evidence adequacy and occurrence establishment remain governed by exact `E` and any separately lawful contract-owned determination.

3. Evidence allocated to `O` must not migrate into evidence for `P`.

4. The occurrence of `C` does not establish the truth, currentness, standing, authority, adoption, or integration of `P`.

5. Expected, failed, surprising, refused, incomplete, or otherwise negative results remain possible lawful occurrence postures. Their content does not change the allocation boundary.

6. Successful enactment does not imply a successful proposition, candidate adoption, or candidate integration.

7. The following inferences are forbidden:

   - it ran, therefore it continues;
   - it worked, therefore it governs;
   - it produced `R`, therefore `R` stands;
   - `R` supports `P`, therefore `P` stands;
   - it worked repeatedly, therefore adopt or integrate it.

8. Trace may persist as evidence; execution authority may not persist through that trace.

9. Invocation, execution, output, result, success, and trace create no successor authorization or reusable permission.

10. Standing lawfully established through a separate lineage is neither granted nor revoked by this boundary.

Allocation is claim-local. It does not evaluate the content of `R`, infer success from `R`, infer proposition support from success, or infer authority from repetition.

## 9. Occurrence-Evidence Contract Discipline

The applicable occurrence-evidence contract remains authoritative over:

- which evidence kinds may support exact `O`;
- what evidence adequacy means;
- whether a contract-owned occurrence posture has been established;
- the scope, rank, custody, and limitations of that posture; and
- the non-claims that posture must preserve.

This boundary may validate only the supplied envelope's completeness, exact identity binding, attribution, contract reference, evidence references, lineage, scope compatibility, allocation target, and non-claims.

This boundary must not:

- invent occurrence evidence;
- evaluate evidence adequacy independently;
- establish `O`;
- reinterpret `E` without admitted basis;
- create a cross-family evidence vocabulary;
- normalize evidence contracts into a global outcome allowlist;
- override the semantic owner of `E`;
- infer contract support from file existence, latest posture, timestamp, sequence, repository availability, or convenience; or
- turn a contract-owned support posture into evidence for `P`.

If `E` is missing, unreadable, ambiguous, unsupported, outside scope, or requires interpretation not admitted by the supplied basis, allocation must stop visibly.

## 10. Outcome Family

The boundary-local outcome family is exactly:

- `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_RECORDED`
- `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_BASIS`
- `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_NOT_RECORDED`
- `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REVIEW_BLOCKED`

`CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_RECORDED` means only that exact supplied evidence references have been allocated to exact `O` under the exact envelope, allocation statement, and contract-owned allocability rule or occurrence-support posture. It does not mean this boundary determined evidence adequacy or established occurrence, proposition truth, standing, authority, adoption, or integration.

`CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_BASIS` means the proposed allocation remains singular, bounded, and non-collapse-shaped, but one or more exact required binding, authorization, contract, result, trace, evidence, lineage, or non-claim facts are absent or incomplete. It creates no request, debt, obligation, schedule, retry, or permission to obtain them.

`CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_NOT_RECORDED` means the readable supplied envelope cannot support the requested allocation while preserving claim locality, exact authorization scope, contract ownership, non-migration, and required non-claims. It does not invalidate `C`, `P`, `A`, `O`, `R`, `T`, or `E`.

`CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REVIEW_BLOCKED` means lawful review cannot proceed because the envelope is missing, malformed, ambiguous, contradictory, unsupported, overreach-shaped, interpreter-authority-shaped, selection-shaped, or migration-shaped.

These are allocation-review outcomes only. `RECORDED` does not mean `ADMITTED`, enacted, verified, true, current, standing, authoritative, adopted, integrated, or successful.

A refused, negative, unsupported, not-recorded, additional-basis, or blocked result is a lawful terminal posture, not operation failure.

## 11. Visible Stopping Conditions

Allocation review must stop visibly when any of the following is missing, ambiguous, contradictory, malformed, unreadable, or unsupported:

- exact `M`;
- exact `C`;
- exact `P`;
- exact `B(C,P)`;
- exact `A` and its admitted scope;
- exact `I`, inputs, and conditions;
- exact `O` and its exact wording;
- exact `R` or `T` references where claimed;
- exact `E` and its applicability to `O`;
- occurrence-evidence references;
- allocation statement;
- exact allocation-to-`O` and non-allocation-to-`P` posture;
- required lineage or custody references; or
- required non-claims.

Review must also stop when completing the requested allocation would require:

- evaluating `P`;
- treating evidence of `O` as evidence for `P`;
- inventing occurrence evidence;
- interpreting a source-family or occurrence-evidence contract without admitted basis;
- choosing among candidates;
- selecting a preferred candidate;
- authorizing enactment;
- authorizing adoption, continuation, or integration;
- migrating evidence to another claim, matter, candidate, or use;
- inferring occurrence from output, result, success, trace, repetition, repository presence, or recency;
- creating a standing invocation lane or reusable authorization;
- granting or revoking separately established standing; or
- creating interpreter authority or semantic ownership.

Where the envelope remains bounded but lacks an exact required fact, `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_BASIS` may be recorded.

Where a readable complete envelope fails allocation locality or contract-owned applicability, `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_NOT_RECORDED` may be recorded.

Where lawful review itself cannot proceed, `CANDIDATE_OCCURRENCE_EVIDENCE_ALLOCATION_REVIEW_BLOCKED` must be recorded.

No stopping posture is operation failure. No stopping posture mutates, repairs, invalidates, adopts, integrates, or reinterprets any selected object.

## 14. Relation to Authorization, Execution, Result, Success, and Trace

Authorization `A` must be separately admitted before it may be cited. This specification does not create, expand, renew, reopen, or consume `A`.

The cited invocation and occurrence remain distinct. Invocation identity does not establish occurrence. Execution, output, result, success, and trace do not establish proposition `P`.

Exact `R` remains result posture only. Exact `T` remains trace posture only. A successful result, repeated result, or persistent trace creates no currentness, standing, authority, successor authorization, adoption force, integration force, reusable permission, or continuation.

If `A` is one-shot or consumed under its owning lineage, allocation does not reopen it. If `A` permits no repetition, trace persistence does not create repetition permission.

Invocation, execution, output, result, success, and trace remain non-self-authorizing.

## 15. Relation to Candidate Evaluation and Proposition Meaning

Candidate material and occurrence trace are not automatically evaluation basis.

The allocation statement must not contain a proposition verdict, evaluation result, candidate-sufficiency result, candidate preference, adoption decision, or integration decision.

The represented proposition's semantic owner remains authoritative over its exact statement and meaning. This boundary does not validate, invalidate, normalize, reinterpret, strengthen, weaken, or replace `P`.

An expected, failed, surprising, refused, incomplete, or negative `R` may still accompany a lawfully bounded occurrence claim. The content of `R` does not widen the evidence allocation beyond `O`.
